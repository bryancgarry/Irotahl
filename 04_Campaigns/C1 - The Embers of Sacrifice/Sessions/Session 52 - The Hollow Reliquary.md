## DM Lore

> [!info] DM Note
> Background for you. The players learn pieces of this through the intro, the murals, and Malcanthet herself.

### Why the Fire Came

During the Sundering, the Abyss drove a wedge into the Elemental Plane of Fire, and on a stolen shore of that plane it raised the Hollow Reliquary. It was the **Seat of the Vessel**: where he was enthroned between battles, worshipped by pilgrims, and filled again.

Fire is chaos, and the Vessel drank chaos. On the Plane of Fire he walked across a great burning reach called the **Ashen Reach**, and where he walked, the flames went out and turned to grey stone. Whole salamander nations were stilled in a single march. The salamanders, the efreet, and the fire's other children laid siege to the Seat.

The siege was led by a young salamander war-sage named **Vistro**, first of his line. His sages bound the Reliquary in a ring of living fire. Then the Sundering broke the world. The Reliquary's anchor to the Abyss ripped it loose and flung it through space and time into the stone beneath what would become Durnhollow. The ring of fire was bound to it, and so were the sages holding it, and the vanguard of the siege came with them.

They melted the cavern into a lava lake to survive and kept the ring burning. It began as a siege. It became a prison. **What they really hold in is the fragment of the Void Heart** kept at the Seat: breaking it would spill the void inside it into the world, and nothing they have could clean that up. A ring of fire can hold it in. Vistro IV is the fourth of that name.

### Who the Vessel Was

- **Naki-Uthai** was a son of Annam All-Father and Othea, the youngest and brightest of the giant princes, the Brave Climber.
- **The betrayal.** During the Sundering, his brother **Memnor**, god of pride and control, bargained with Malcanthet for power. The price was Naki-Uthai, delivered to her as a gift.
- **The hollowing.** Malcanthet hollowed him in the Soul Sanctum (see below), then sent him against **Ysgard**, the Shield-Halls of the giants, against his own kin. He drank giant souls on every field.
- **The leash.** His mother Othea had given him a pendant that he wore on every climb. Malcanthet broke it open, filled it with the Sea of Void, and hung it back around his neck. It kept his mind hollow and answering to her. Now it is in pieces: the **Void Heart**. One fragment rests at the Seat.
- **The erasure.** Annam struck his name from the Ordning in grief and shame, and Memnor's part was buried with it. The Goliaths still honor **Naki-Uthai, the Brave Climber**, as their god of bravery and climbing, with no idea he was ever real or ever the Vessel.
- **The turn (hold).** The giant souls inside him remembered his name, the one Malcanthet took, and in time he came back to himself and turned on his makers. The Reliquary never shows this.

### The Hollowing

Hollowing is Malcanthet's art, perfected by her hollowers in the Soul Sanctum. It works on all three parts of a being:
- **The body** is stilled into a shell of stone, so the vessel can't be broken.
- **The mind** is pierced inward with needles of void until every word and thought in it is cut out, so the vessel doesn't question. *(The Soulknife in reverse: blades going in instead of out.)*
- **The soul** is drawn out, lowered into the Sea of Void until it turns dark, and set back, so the vessel is hers.

**The Sea of Void** moves like black ferrofluid: liquid that pools, beads, and rises into bristling spikes and needles. It reaches for thought.

> [!info] DM Note
> The Sea of Void seeps from the bottom of the Abyss, where the Shard of Evil sits: a sliver of the Eternal Void. No one in the world knows this.

### The Soul Sanctum

- The Soul Sanctum was Malcanthet's house of making in the Abyss, where her hollowers perfected hollowing and where Naki-Uthai was hollowed. The Reliquary honors the Vessel. The Sanctum made him.
- Like the Reliquary, it was torn loose in the Sundering and flung through the planes. It came to rest in the Drak'mor mountains, half buried in the stone. To the Goliaths it is only old ruins.
- **These are the ruins where Wayne was turned to stone.** Its sigil, a triangle point down with a pupil-less eye at its center, is carved over a doorway there.
- What still waits inside is open. Scripture says the hollowers "keep their vigil still."

**Wayne leaves with this.** The Podium beat (Section 5) is guaranteed: the party can't go further without the stone book, so Wayne learns the Sanctum's name and sees its sigil even if every trial fails.

### The Tome of Malcanthet

- The Tome was **never** in the Reliquary. It was held by the Holy Silver Chain Knights before Thimble came to carry it.
- It holds a piece of Malcanthet's own layer of the Abyss, which keeps it updated with everything she is. She hates that it exists.
- **Vashara learned hollowing here**, from the Reliquary's own stone scripture book on the podium. She also found her **Ember** inside the Reliquary. She opened the doors the first time the old way: with an offering of her own blood.

## Intro Scene: The Siege at the Edge of Fire

> A cold open from the Sundering, before returning to Atul. Run it one of two ways:
> - **Narrated** (5 minutes): read the descriptions straight through.
> - **Played** (15 to 20 minutes): each player takes a salamander warrior of the siege for a few rounds. Use the **Salamander** statblock. The point is the spectacle and the loss, not winning.
>
> Don't tell the players where or when this is. Let them work it out once they see the temple.

**DM Description: The Siege**
> A sea of fire stretches to every horizon. Not a fire burning on something. Fire is the ground, the air, the sky. Rivers of white flame pour upward into storms of orange cloud, and towers of living magma rise and fall like breathing.
>
> And on a black shore in the middle of it, where the flames go dull and sick, stands a building that should not be there. Black stone, so dark the firelight slides off it. Gothic arches. One tall spire. Around it, a ring of fire unlike the rest: ordered, rhythmic, pulsing in time with a chant.
>
> You are part of that ring. You are salamanders: long serpentine bodies of red scale and flame, spears of burning iron in your hands, and around you are thousands more. Efreet with scimitars of smoke stand in the burning air. Fire elementals roar at the walls. On basalt pillars, the war-sages coil and chant, holding the ring in place, and at their center, young and fierce, is the sage they call **Vistro**.
>
> "Break the Seat," he calls, and the whole sea of fire answers him. "For the Ashen Reach. For our dead."

**If played:** Give each player one or two rounds of attacking the walls and the hollowed things that defend them (grey, silent, stone pilgrims; no stats needed, they shatter on a hit).

**DM Description: The Vessel Rises**
> The temple doors open.
>
> Something steps out that is far too large for them, and yet it fits. A giant, made of black stone from crown to heel, wearing a crown of a style that belongs to no demon. At his throat hangs a small pale pendant, and in its cracked setting, a bead of black moves on its own. His chest is open, a hollow where his heart should be. His face is calm. His eyes are empty.
>
> He walks into the ring of fire.
>
> And the fire goes out.
>
> Not extinguished. Stilled. Where he steps, the flames stop moving, harden, and turn grey. The salamanders in front of him freeze mid-strike, their bodies of flame becoming stone mid-coil. The efreet above him fall out of the sky as statues. The silence spreads from his feet like frost across a window, and he does not hurry, and he does not stop.
>
> Vistro screams for the sages to hold the ring. They hold it.

**DM Description: The Sundering**
> Then the sky tears.
>
> Not the sky of fire. Something beneath it, behind it, the frame the whole world hangs on. A sound like a mountain splitting goes through everything at once. The black temple lurches, its spire swinging wide, and you see it begin to fall, not down, but *away*, as if something far below has seized it by the roots and is pulling.
>
> The ring of fire is bound to it. The sages are bound to the ring. Vistro grips his pillar and does not let go, and the temple drags the ring, the sages, and the vanguard of the siege with it, out of the fire, down through a darkness with no bottom.
>
> The last thing you see is the stone giant, standing at the doors, turning his calm, empty face toward you.

**Transition**
> And then you are yourselves again, standing on an island of black glass in a lava lake under the earth, before that same temple, leaning a few degrees off true. Around you, the ring of fire still roars, held by the chanting of sages who have never stopped. In front of you, a dwarf king made of stone draws his first breath in a very long time.

**Notes**
- Thimble's crimson Vistro seal may warm during the transition.
- If a player asks, the salamander they played has no name they can recall. Some of them are still in the Reliquary, stone, at the Apex.

## Running the Reliquary

**The Danger.** The salamanders hold the fire for two reasons.
- **It hollows the living.** For every hour spent on the island or inside, each creature makes a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure, it gains 1 Stone. Stone from the Pull fades after a long rest outside the barrier.
  - 1 Stone: speed reduced by 10 feet
  - 2 Stone: disadvantage on Dexterity checks and saves
  - 3 Stone: can't take reactions, speed halved
  - 4 Stone: petrified (stone from within)
- **The Abyss leaks through it.** At the Apex, the seam to the Abyss is thin.

**The Brands.** The party's Abyssal Brands react to the Reliquary: warm on the island, cold when a faithful path is taken, burning at the Apex (real pain, no damage). Bogtrotter has no brand, so he feels none of this.

**The Structure.** Outside, the Nave, the Turning (gravity flips), three trial chambers in the inverted spire (Body, Mind, Soul), then the Apex. Each trial awards one **sigil piece**. All three open the Apex.

**The Trials.** Each trial has a **faithful path**, the Abyss's intended answer, which works but costs something lasting, and other ways through for a clever or stubborn party.
- **Body:** fight your own hollow shell.
- **Mind:** solve a puzzle with your words cut out.
- **Soul:** become the blade, and see what's left of you.

**Natural stopping points:** after Atul (Section 1), after the stone book (Section 5), after the Body trial (Section 7), after the Soul trial (Section 9).

## 3. The Walls and the Doors

**DM Description (the walls)**
> Up close, the temple's walls writhe with carvings: demonic shapes wrapped around one another, too many limbs, too many mouths, all turned toward a single figure repeated over and over in the stone. A giant, crowned, with a hollow in his chest where a heart should be. Between the carvings, small offering niches are cut into the stone at kneeling height.

**Rolls (the walls)**
- **Religion or Arcana DC 13:** The script is Abyssal. Beneath the hollow giant, the same phrase repeats: *The First Vessel. The Hollow One. The Mother's Masterwork.*
- **History DC 15:** The carved crown is not a demon's crown. It's in the style of the ancient giant courts.
- **Anyone who played the intro (no roll):** This is the giant from the siege.

**DM Description (the doors)**
> The doors are twice the height of a giant: two slabs of lightless stone without visible hinges or handles. Dense spirals of Abyssal script wind inward to a single sigil where the doors meet: a heart split down the middle. The left half is carved solid and full. The right half is only an outline, the stone inside cut away into a shallow basin. Around it coils a ring of lilac blossoms, each petal carved so finely it looks soft. Where the two halves meet, the stone is smooth and worn, as if a great many hands have been pressed there.

**The Inscription Above the Doors** *(translated)*
> *THE HOLLOW RELIQUARY. Here is kept the Seat of the First Vessel, who was emptied that the deep might be filled. Pilgrim, fall. Pilgrim, give. Pilgrim, become as he became.*

**The Script on the Doors** *(translated)*
> *He gave his flesh, his mind, and his soul, and was made holy. Pilgrim, give as he gave, and enter his house.*

**How it opens: an offering of self**
- A pilgrim places a piece of themselves in the hollow half of the heart (blood, hair, a tooth, a fingernail) while pressing their palm to the worn seam. The stone drinks it, and the doors swing inward without a sound.
- Blood costs **1d4 necrotic damage** that can't be healed until a long rest. The brand stings as it's taken.
- One pilgrim's offering opens the doors for everyone.

**Clues**
- **Investigation DC 12:** The hollow half of the heart is stained dark, layer on layer, centuries deep.
- **Perception DC 13:** The offering niches on the walls aren't empty. They hold carved stone fingers, braided locks of hair turned to stone, and small teeth set in rows.
- **Religion DC 13:** Demon pilgrims never brought gold to a holy place. They brought what was theirs.
- **Atul's account:** her hand pressed to the stone, bleeding.

**Wrong offerings:** gold, gems, or someone else's blood are spat back out of the basin, and a gargoyle on the wall turns its head. A third wrong offering, or any attempt to force the doors, wakes the guardians.

**The Guardians**
- **3 Gargoyles** (Monster Manual), peeling off the walls, and **1 Basilisk** (Monster Manual), uncoiling from the black glass.
- The basilisk's stone is "the lesser art," the kind the stone book inside dismisses.
- **Wayne:** he's already stone. The basilisk's gaze lands on him and finds nothing left to take. Let that be a moment.
- Once the guardians fall, the doors are just heavy doors. They push open with effort (Strength DC 15, or two people).

## 4. The Nave of Pilgrims

**DM Description**
> The inside is bigger than the outside. Not by much, but enough that you notice.
>
> You stand in a vast gothic nave, its ribbed vaults lost in darkness overhead. The floor is polished black stone, worn into shallow paths by countless feet, all leading toward the center. >
> Before the shaft stands a lectern of black stone, and on it lies a book with pages of stone, carved thin as slate.
>
> The air smells of incense centuries stale. Under the quiet, you hear a slow, deep pulse, like a heartbeat, rising from the shaft.
>
> Around the nave, the walls break into spaces for pilgrims: a long offering hall, a ring of prayer alcoves, and everywhere between them, murals.

**Rolls**

- **Arcana DC 14 (at the shaft's edge):** The pull at the edge is wrong. Gravity here doesn't point down. It points *in*.

**Notes**

### 4c. The War Murals

**DM Description (read as a walking tour)**

> **The first panel.** A sky split in three. To the left, the Shield-Halls: mountains floating over rivers of fire, and on them giants in their halls, shields raised. In the center, the Bright Courts: golden forests and tall antlered figures carrying blades of four colors, green, gold, blue, and silver. To the right, the Wild Fields, thick with beasts. Below them all, the mortal peoples of the world, and below everything, a churning dark full of shapes that have no right to exist. > **The second panel.** The armies of the dark pour upward. At their head stride five vast figures: a spider-shape of impossible joints, a horned and bestial thing, a swollen thing crowned in bone, a long coiling shape, and one beautiful figure, a woman with wings folded like a cloak. The caption: *The Five rose from the deep to claim what was theirs.*

> **The third panel.** The war goes badly. On the floating mountains, the giants hold the line, shields locked, and beside them the antlered knights charge with their colored blades. The dark recoils. The caption: *The giants of the Shield-Halls stood like walls, and the deep broke upon them.*

> **The fourth panel.** At the center of the dark army, the beautiful winged figure stands with her arms open, receiving something. A giant kneels before her, crowned, his head bowed. His face is carved with great care. He is beautiful too. The caption: *And to the Mother was given a gift no demon had ever been given. And she made of it the Vessel, and the tide turned.*

> **The fifth panel.** The stone giant walking the floating mountains of the Shield-Halls. Before him, giants lower their shields and kneel to their lost prince, and behind him, those who knelt are grey and still. The caption: *They knelt to the prince they remembered. He did not remember them.*

> **The sixth panel.** A sea of fire, and the stone giant walking through it, the flames turning grey and still behind him. Serpentine figures of fire are frozen around his feet. The caption: *Even the fire knelt.*

**Rolls**
- **History DC 14:** The Shield-Halls is an old demonic name for Ysgard, the plane of giants and endless battle. The Bright Courts are Arborea, and the antlered knights are eladrin. The Wild Fields are the Beastlands. This is the Sundering, told from the Abyss's side.
- **History DC 12 (Alaric especially):** The eladrin blades are colored by season: green for spring, gold for summer, blue for autumn, silver for winter.
- **Religion DC 15:** The Five are obyriths, the first and oldest demons. The winged one is Malcanthet.
- **Insight DC 14:** The murals never show who gave the gift. The panel was carved that way on purpose.
- **Investigation DC 18 (fourth panel):** At the very edge of the carving, half chiseled away, is a second giant's hand, open, as if it has just let go of something. *(Memnor. Do not name him.)*
- **Sixth panel (no roll):** The frozen figures are salamanders. Anyone who played the intro recognizes the scene.

## 5. The Podium and the Stone Book

**DM Description**
> The book's pages are slabs of black stone, thin as slate, hinged with iron. They turn heavily, with a sound like a tomb being opened. Every page is carved in dense Abyssal script. The first page holds a prayer, and a dark handprint has been worn into its corner, where a great many hands have rested.

**Reading the book**
- It's written in Abyssal. Anyone who reads Abyssal, or casts *comprehend languages*, can read it.
- Otherwise, **Intelligence (Arcana or Religion) DC 14** sounds out the prayer well enough to speak it, without fully understanding it.

**The Prayer** *(the first page)*
> *Hollow One, First Vessel,*
> *emptied by her, filled by her,*
> *I give what is mine.*
> *Let me fall as you fell.*
> *Open.*

**DM Description (when the prayer is spoken aloud)**
> The heartbeat stops. Total silence.
>
> Around the nave, every carving of the hollow giant turns its stone face toward the lectern. Then, from the shaft, the heartbeat starts again, louder. And the pull at the edge grows stronger, as if the dark below has noticed you.

**The Making of the Vessel** *(the page that matters)*
> *Hollowing is not the stilling of flesh into stone, as the lesser art of the basilisk and the medusa. The lesser art seals from without, and the stone sleeps. Hollowing is done in three parts. The body is stilled into a shell of stone, so the vessel cannot be broken. The mind is pierced with needles of the Sea until every word in it is cut away, so the vessel does not question. The soul is drawn out, made dark in the Sea, and set back, so the vessel is hers alone. This is the art of the Soul Sanctum, the Mother's house of making, where her hollowers keep their vigil.*

**DM Description (the sigil)**
> Beneath the words, where a scribe would set a seal, is a mark that is not the Mother's split heart. A plain triangle, point down. At its center, an open eye with no pupil.

**Wayne (no roll)**
> You have seen this mark before. It was carved over a doorway in the old ruins in the Drak'mor mountains, worn and half buried, and you walked under it without a second thought. It was the last thing you saw before the stone.

**Rolls**
- **Arcana DC 13:** The "lesser art" is ordinary petrification. This is something else entirely, which is why ordinary cures wouldn't work on it.
- **Insight DC 12 (Wayne):** A body stilled into a shell of stone. That sounds a lot like you.
- **Religion DC 13:** The page speaks of the Soul Sanctum the way pilgrims speak of a holy city. The Reliquary is where the Vessel is honored. The Sanctum is where he was made.
- **Investigation DC 14 (the margins):** Faint graphite notes in Dwarvish are scratched along the edges of the Making page, in Vashara's hand. She studied it here, line by line.

**Notes**

## 6. The Turning

**DM Description (stepping off the edge)**
> You fall.
>
> The dark rushes up around you, cold and total. The heartbeat is everywhere now, in the stone, in your ribs. You fall for a long time. Longer than the island is deep.
>
> Then, somewhere in the middle of the dark, the world turns over. Your stomach flips. The rushing air slows, stops, and reverses. You are no longer falling down. You are falling *up*, slowing, slowing, until your feet touch stone as gently as stepping off a stair.
>
> You are standing on a ceiling. Above you, where the floor should be, the shaft you jumped from is a small pale circle, far overhead. Around you rises a vast, narrowing cathedral of black stone: the inside of the spire. And it goes *down*. Every stair, every buttress, every window points toward the dark beneath your feet.
>
> You have been climbing the spire since the moment you jumped.

**How it works**
- Gravity inside the spire points toward its tip. Every floor the party "descends" carries them higher up the spire.
- Loose objects drift lazily toward the tip. Water runs "down" the walls toward the tip.
- Climbing back out means climbing "up" toward the shaft, then falling through the Turning again.

**Rolls**
- **Arcana DC 13:** The spire isn't upside down. You are. From inside, the Abyss is the sky.
- **Religion DC 12:** The Abyssal faith in miniature: to rise, you fall.
- **Wayne (Goliath, no roll):** Every Goliath grows up on Tyrn'Haka, the Endless Climb. This place is its mirror. An endless fall, dressed as a climb.

### 6a. The Inverted Stair *(optional exploration)*

**DM Description**
> A great spiral stair winds down the inside of the spire wall toward the first chamber. Partway down, the stair seems to bend the wrong way. Through narrow windows, you can see the lava lake glowing beneath your feet, so close and so far you have to look away.

**How it plays**
- At the midpoint, gravity tilts 90 degrees for a stretch. The stair becomes a wall, and the wall becomes the floor.
- **Athletics or Acrobatics DC 13** to cross the tilt cleanly. On a failure, the creature drifts toward the spire's open center and must be caught (an ally's reaction) or take 2d6 bludgeoning damage slamming into a buttress.
- Clever answers work: tying everyone together, walking along the wall, Bogtrotter running along the buttresses.

## 7. The Chamber of Body: The Stilling

**Map**
- Circular room about 60 ft across, open central shaft about 15 ft across, stair arriving at the south edge
- Smooth black floor, no hazards. The walls are the threat.

**DM Description (entering)**
> A circular chamber ringed around the open shaft. The floor is bare, polished black stone. On the walls, all the way around, is the mural.
>
> As the last of you steps into the chamber, the walls ripple like water. Out of the black stone step figures: you. Every scar, every buckle, every way you hold your weapon. But they're carved from the same lightless stone as the walls, and where each one's heart should be, there's a hollow. Their eyes are empty. They don't speak. They raise their weapons exactly the way you would.

**The Mural of the Body**

> **The first panel.** A giant prince in the fullness of his strength, crowned, standing on a mountaintop beneath an open sky. A small pendant hangs at his throat. Behind him, a towering, bearded figure of light raises a hand in blessing, and beside it, a tall woman smiles. The caption: *He was the greatest of the sons of the All-Father, and the world knew his name.*

> **The second panel.** The prince lying on an altar in the dark, his eyes open, his face calm. The Mother stands over him, one hand resting on his chest. The caption: *And he was given to the Mother, and he did not resist.*

> **The third panel.** The largest carving in the chamber. Stone growing through the prince from the inside out: from the center of his chest, spreading through the ribs, down the arms, up the throat. His flesh pours out of him in carved rivulets and runs down the altar. The caption: *She poured out his flesh, and grew the stone of the deep in its place, so that he could never be wounded.*

> **The fourth panel.** The giant rising from the altar, stone from crown to heel, a hollow in his chest. The caption: *A shell that cannot break. A body that cannot tire. So may you be.*

**The Hollow Shadows**
- **Stats:** each shadow uses its character's own sheet: AC, attacks, spells, and features. It has the same resources the character has *as they enter the room*.
- **Half hit points:** each shadow has half its character's maximum hit points.
- **Hollow:** immune to being charmed, frightened, and petrified. They can't be reasoned with.
- **Stone body:** vulnerable to bludgeoning damage. They crack and shatter.

**The trick**
- Each shadow hunts **its own original** and ignores everyone else unless attacked.
- A shadow knows everything its original knows, so it **has advantage on attacks against its original.** Against anyone else, it fights blind: **other characters have advantage on attacks against it.**
- The winning move is to swap partners. **Insight DC 13** at the end of the first round, or anyone who notices the pattern, can spot this.
- **Myra** has no shadow and can help anyone.

**Wayne's shadow:** it's what he would be if he were finished. At its throat hangs a small pale pendant with a bead of black inside. *(Foreshadowing the Void Heart.)*

**Dialog**
- **The Reliquary (as the shadows step out):** "Bear what he bore."
- **The Reliquary (to Wayne, privately):** "You are already halfway."

**Success:** the last shadow shatters, and the first sigil piece lies in the rubble of its chest.

**Failure:** a character who drops to 0 hit points here doesn't make death saving throws. Instead, they turn to stone from the chest outward until the fight ends. If the whole party falls, they wake on the stair outside, each with 1 Stone, and the shadows wait for them to come back.

## 8. The Chamber of Mind: The Silencing

**Map**
- Same circular footprint
- Five stone pillars spaced evenly around the room, each topped with a rotating stone dial and carved with one party member's face
- A single iron lever in the center, at the shaft's edge
- The ceiling is a pool of the Sea of Void

**DM Description (entering)**
> The ceiling of this chamber is a pool of black liquid, glassy and still, that should pour down on you and doesn't. Around the room stand five stone pillars, each topped with a rotating stone dial, and each carved with a face. Yours. All five of you, in stone, eyes closed. In the center stands a single iron lever.
>
> Then the black overhead stirs. It rises into needles thinner than hair, thousands of them, and they come for you.

**The Mural of the Mind**

> **The first panel.** The Mother before the stone giant, a crown of black needles rising from her fingertips. *A mind is a house full of words, and every word is a door that does not lead to her.*

> **The second panel.** The needles pierce his brow, one by one, and the words pour out of him like birds leaving a tree. *She closed every door.*

> **The third panel.** His mind dark and silent. With a single needle, she writes one bright mark into the emptiness. *And in the silence, she wrote her will.*

> **The fourth panel.** The giant striding into battle, perfectly silent, perfectly sure. *He needed no words. He had hers.*

**The Needles**
Each pilgrim feels a cold point at their brow. No pain. But afterward:
- **Words are gone.** No speech, no writing (letters smear and drift apart), no telepathy. Spells with verbal components can't be cast. This lasts until they leave the chamber.
- **Table rule:** players can't talk to each other about the puzzle. Gestures, pointing, and miming only. You narrate what their characters do.

**The puzzle**
- Each dial shows the same five carved symbols: **an eye, a hand, a mouth, a chain, and a crown.**
- Each needle leaves one image in its pilgrim's emptied mind, and it's always the answer for **someone else's** pillar. Hand each player their card secretly.
- When all five dials are set, someone pulls the lever.

**The secret images** *(one card per player; a closed loop so everyone has to communicate)*
- **Aalyn's card:** Alaric's pillar, turned to **the eye**.
- **Alaric's card:** Bogtrotter's pillar, turned to **the chain**.
- **Bogtrotter's card:** Thimble's pillar, turned to **the hand**.
- **Thimble's card:** Wayne's pillar, turned to **the crown**.
- **Wayne's card:** Aalyn's pillar, turned to **the mouth**.

*(If a player is absent, give their card to the player whose pillar it describes, so the loop still closes.)*

**A wrong setting:** the dials spin back, and more needles come. Each creature takes **2d8 psychic damage** (DC 14 Wisdom saving throw for half) as the void pushes deeper.

**The faithful path:** let a needle go all the way in. That pilgrim hears the Mother's voice clearly, and she tells them every setting. *Cost:* a real memory, and disadvantage on Intelligence saving throws until their next long rest. *(They still have to act on it without words.)*

**Clever outs**
- Myra's rapport spores carry feelings and images, not words. The void may not be able to stop them. Your call.
- Drawing pictures works (only letters fail). Pointing at a pillar's carved face, then miming the symbol, is the intended solution.

**Success:** the lever pulls, the needles withdraw into the ceiling, and words come flooding back. The second sigil piece drops from the black pool into the lever's base.

## 9. The Chamber of Soul: The Descent of the Blade

**Map**
- Same circular footprint
- The open shaft is filled to the brim with the Sea of Void
- A black stone greatsword stands point-first in the surface of the void, its hilt within reach of the shaft's edge

**DM Description (entering)**
> The shaft here isn't empty. It's full to the brim with black liquid, glassy and still, a well with no bottom. Standing in it, driven point-first into its surface as if it were stone, is a greatsword of black stone taller than a giant. Its hilt is wrapped in grey leather gone hard as rock. Words are carved down the length of its blade.
>
> As you come near, each of you sees a faint pale light glowing in your own chest, white as bone and warm as a hearth. In everyone else's, too.

**Carved down the blade** *(translated)*
> *A blade does not hate. A blade does not grieve. A blade does not hesitate. And so, a blade may do what no living soul could ever bear.*

**The Mural of the Soul**

> **The first panel.** A black sky beyond the stars. From it falls a single pale seed, and the one who catches it becomes the Deep. *The first offering was made before any altar.*

> **The second panel.** The Deep weeps small pale stones up into the world. A figure holds one aloft, and around them kneel those they offered, each bearing a mark on their skin. *Those offered are marked, and the mark is a promise: what is pale shall be made dark.* *(The marks are carved exactly like the party's brands.)*

> **The third panel.** A pale hall of hollowers, each with a triangle and a pupil-less eye stitched on their robes. The stone giant is led in among them, quiet and willing. *In the Sanctum, the Mother's hollowers made ready her beloved.*

> **The fourth panel.** The hollowers draw a pale, curled soul out of his open chest on a thread of light, and hold it up in a cage of glass. *What is pale must be emptied.*

> **The fifth panel.** The pale soul lowered into a black sea, raised again black as the sea itself, and set back into the hollow of his chest. *So was his soul made dark, and made hers.*

**Rolls (murals)**
- **Religion DC 14 (second panel):** Those marks are your brands. Whatever the pale stones are, they're tied to them.
- **Arcana DC 15 (first and second panels):** The pale stones the Deep weeps sound like the Embers of Sacrifice, the stone Atul described.
- **Insight DC 13:** "What is pale shall be made dark." It reads like a promise about *your* souls.

**The ritual**
The pilgrims lay their hands on the hilt together, and the blade sinks into the void, taking them with it. There's no drowning, only darkness, then memory. They are *him*, inside the Vessel's memory, at his three stations of stripping.

**The three depths** *(each a memory of the Shield-Halls)*
1. **Hate.** A giant chieftain stands in his burning hall and curses you, the hollow thing wearing a prince's shape, as a traitor to his own blood. Strike him down. *A blade does not hate.*
2. **Grief.** The field after. Giant souls pour out of the fallen and into your chest, and around you, the living kneel among their dead and wail. Walk through them. *A blade does not grieve.*
3. **Hesitation.** The last of them: a giant shield-maiden alone in a doorway, and behind her, the hall where the young are hidden. Raise your hand. *A blade does not hesitate.*

**The choice** *(made secretly at each depth, with face-down Blade / Self cards)*
- **Become the blade.** Let it go. Your light dims one step, and the void lets you pass untouched.
- **Remain yourself.** Hold on. Your light stays, but the void bears down on you: a **Wisdom saving throw** (DC 13, then 15, then 17), taking **2d8, then 3d8, then 4d8 psychic damage** on a failure.

**What remains**
At the bottom, in total darkness, the void asks each character one question: *"What is left of you?"* Each player answers in a sentence, in character. Then everyone sees everyone's light, bright, dim, or gone, for the first time.

**The outcome**
The trial passes no matter what. The blade rises out of the void and slides free, sinking away into the dark below. Where its point was buried, the third sigil piece floats on the surface: pale when they first touched the hilt, black as void now.
- **Characters whose light went out** (three Blade cards) surface as perfect blades. For the rest of the Reliquary, they have advantage on saving throws against being charmed or frightened, and their weapon attacks deal an extra 1d6 necrotic damage. *Cost:* their brand darkens permanently, and Malcanthet knows their name.
- **Characters who held on** keep themselves, wounds and all.

**Wayne:** his light gutters at every depth, but it never goes out, no matter what he chooses. The Reliquary doesn't explain why.

**The Tome:** if Thimble carries the Tome of Malcanthet during the descent, it drinks the light he gives up. Malcanthet feels it, and arrives at the end knowing who has her Tome.

**Aalyn:** at the bottom, before the question, the void asks him first: *"You are already promised. To whom?"*

## 15. The Fragment of the Void Heart

**The choice.** No one makes Wayne take it. Let the table talk it through. Malcanthet saying "take it, or don't" makes it harder, not easier. If Wayne doesn't take it, anyone else can, but the curse only bites a would-be vessel. For anyone but Wayne it's an inert, faintly warm stone.

**The item** *(D&D Beyond-ready)*
```
FRAGMENT OF THE VOID HEART
Wondrous item, very rare (requires attunement). Cursed.

A shard of a giant's pendant, grey stone worn smooth by a climber's hand, its setting cracked open. Inside, a bead of black void moves on its own, rising into tiny spikes whenever someone nearby thinks too hard.

Shade Step. As a bonus action, you dissolve into living shadow and move up to your speed in a straight line. During this move, you can pass through other creatures' spaces, and you don't provoke opportunity attacks. Each creature you pass through must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw (DC 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Dexterity modifier) or take 2d6 necrotic damage. You can use Shade Step a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest. Each use beyond that gives you 1 Hollowing.

Kin of the Void. The Sea of Void doesn't bristle at you. Hollowed creatures hesitate before attacking you: the first attack each one makes against you in a combat has disadvantage.

Curse. Once you attune to the fragment, you can't remove it or end your attunement, and remove curse has no effect on it. Only breaking Malcanthet's claim on you ends the curse.

Hollowing. Each point of Hollowing costs you a memory of the DM's choosing.
- At 3 Hollowing, Malcanthet can whisper to you through the fragment at will. Her words are suggestions only. You can always refuse.
- At 5 Hollowing, your mind is hollowed. Once per long rest, Malcanthet can cast command on you through the fragment, with no saving throw.

Homebrew note: Shade Step and Kin of the Void are homebrew, inspired by Hollow Knight's Shade Cloak, Sharp Shadow, and Void Heart. The curse follows the DMG's cursed item conventions.
```

**The path forward** *(DM only)*
Each further fragment Wayne finds adds power and tightens the leash. The complete **Void Heart** turns his psychic blades into blades of void that deal necrotic damage, makes hollowed creatures stop treating him as an enemy, and lets Malcanthet speak to him without any Hollowing at all.
