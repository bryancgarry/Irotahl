## 0. Cold Open: The Void Trial

> [!info] DM Note
> Wayne's player only, as a played memory. The Shell pulses once in his hand, and the memory he's never had comes back all at once. **Malcanthet is opening it for him**, but nobody knows that until she speaks at the end.
> Run it in four beats: **the send-off**, **the descent**, **the voices in the dark**, and **the battlefield**. The rolls color the memory; Wayne can't fail out of it, because he already lived it. A failed check means it cost him something then, and he feels the echo now (a little psychic damage, nothing lasting), or it changes what he notices.
> The other players can watch. If anyone has a reason to reach in (Thimble's psionic link, especially), let them glimpse it.

> [!readaloud] The Shell pulses
> The Shell in your hand gives one slow, heavy beat. Like a heart that isn't yours.
>
> And you're not at the summit anymore.

### Beat 1: The Send-Off

> [!readaloud]
> Wind. Cold, thin mountain wind, and a sky so close you could touch it. You're standing on the lip of the Rikk Borgar, the great abyss at the heart of Drak'mor, and you're young. Your skin is grey, not stone. Your hands are your own.
>
> Behind you, the Volkarr stand in a half-circle, old Goliath shamans in feathered cloaks with their faces painted white. The Kerr'Athrai have come to watch, too. You can feel them at your back, a whole tribe holding its breath.
>
> In front of you, the floating peaks drift around the abyss in slow circles, like moons around nothing. Below them, there is no bottom. Just dark.
>
> The eldest of the Volkarr puts a hand on your shoulder. "Climb down until there's no more down. Bring back a stone from the bottom. If you come back at all, you come back Kyn'Ruthar."

- **Optional, Wisdom (Insight) DC 13:** the eldest shaman won't meet Wayne's eyes. He's sent many down. Most don't come back. He's afraid for Wayne, and something else: he's afraid *of* what comes back.
- **Wayne's choice:** how he goes over the edge. Climbing, leaping to the nearest floating peak, or simply stepping off. Let him narrate it.

### Beat 2: The Descent

> [!readaloud]
> The first thousand feet are rock. Then the rock gets hot. Seams of red light open in the walls, and the air above them shimmers. The deeper you go, the more the walls are fire with a skin of stone over it, and the stone is thinning.

Three checks, any order. Each describes a stretch of the climb.
- **Strength (Athletics) DC 15:** the long climb down the burning wall. *Failure:* he slips and catches himself hard; 1d6 psychic now as his hands remember the burns.
- **Constitution saving throw DC 14:** the heat. *Failure:* he passes through a sheet of flame he can't avoid; 1d6 psychic now, and the smell of his own burned hair.
- **Dexterity (Acrobatics) DC 14:** a ledge gives way, and he has to fall to the next one. *Success:* he lands like a cat. *Failure:* he lands badly and lies stunned in the dark for a long time, listening to the abyss breathe.

> [!readaloud]
> Then the fire stops. Below the last of it, there's only dark. Not a darkness you can see into. A darkness that's *thick*, like water, like oil, and it moves.

### Beat 3: The Voices in the Dark

> [!readaloud]
> You go down into it.
>
> It's quiet. Then it isn't. Voices come up out of the dark around you, soft at first. Your mother's voice. Your clan's. Your own voice, younger. They all want the same thing.
>
> *Stop. Rest. Let go. You don't have to climb anymore.*
>
> Around your hands, the dark rises into fine black needles, reaching for you, the way the void reached in the Mind trial. Every time you think, they reach a little closer.

- **Wisdom saving throw DC 15**, three times. The voices get closer each time. *Each failure:* 1d6 psychic now, and the voices sound more like his own.
- **Wayne's choice at any point:** fight the voices, ignore them, or answer them. Whatever he does, he keeps climbing down.
- **If he answers them:** they go quiet, satisfied, and the needles lie flat. *(He said yes to something, very young, without knowing what. Malcanthet will remember.)*
- **If he resists all three:** the needles flinch back from him, the way they did when he cleared his mind in the Mind trial. The void is wary of him, even then.
- **Lore, no roll:** at the bottom of the dark, the voices fade, and one remains. A woman's voice, warm and very close, and nothing like his mother's at all.

### Beat 4: The Battlefield

> [!readaloud]
> Your feet touch ground.
>
> The dark thins, and you're standing at the bottom of the Rikk Borgar, the place no one is supposed to reach, and it isn't a pit at all.
>
> It's a battlefield.
>
> Grey earth runs out in every direction, flat and cold. Giant shields lie half-sunk in it like fallen moons. Bones as long as rivers. Broken spears taller than towers. And above you, where there should be rock, there's a *sky*, torn open like cloth, its edges hanging in ragged mountains of stone. The floating peaks that ring the Rikk Borgar. You understand, standing there, that they aren't peaks. They're the edge of another world, ripped away from this one. Something enormous broke off here, and took its sky with it.
>
> In the middle of the field stands a giant made of grey stone.

**What Wayne can learn here** *(let him explore; each roll is a piece of the field)*
- **Intelligence (History) DC 14:** the shields bear the marks of the giant halls of Ysgard. He knows them from the oldest Goliath songs. This was a war of giants.
- **Wisdom (Perception) DC 13:** five long shadows are burned into the grey ground near the stone giant, as if five huge figures stood there in a terrible light. Whatever cast them is gone.
- **Intelligence (Investigation) DC 15:** the giant isn't a statue. He's a moment. He's frozen mid-turn: his blade half-raised against the giants behind him, half-swung around toward the five shadows. His face is caught between two expressions. In his other hand, clutched to him, is a piece of plate, like half of a breastplate.
- **Wisdom (Insight) DC 14:** the giant's face. One expression is perfect, empty obedience. The other is grief. He is in the middle of choosing.

> [!readaloud] Then two things happen at once
> A voice. Hers. Warm and very close. *"There you are. Take it."*
>
> At your feet, a stone, smooth and dark, warm as a living thing. You pick it up. You don't remember deciding to.
>
> And the stone giant moves. Just his eyes. They slide down to you, slow as glaciers, and look at you, and a spark leaps the distance between you, white and small, and goes into your chest.
>
> You climb out of the abyss carrying both.

- **Wayne's last choice, optional:** does he reach for the half-breastplate? If he does, his hand closes on nothing. It isn't time yet. *(It's the first half of [[The Soul Armor]]. Don't name it.)*

> [!readaloud] The return
> Wind again. The lip of the abyss, the Volkarr, the whole tribe roaring your name. The eldest shaman takes the dark stone from your hand and holds it up to the sky and calls you Kyn'Ruthar.
>
> You smile and wave and don't remember a single thing that happened below.
>
> Until now.

> [!secret] What the cold open reveals
> revealed:: true
> - The bottom of the Rikk Borgar is a battlefield of the Sundering, where Ysgard tore away from the material plane.
> - A stone giant stands there frozen in the moment he turned.
> - Malcanthet's voice gave Wayne the stone (her mark). The giant's spark went into him at the same moment. He came out carrying both.
>
> **Still hidden:** that the giant is the First Hollow Vessel and Naki-Uthai, that the plate is the Soul Armor, and that a Kyn'Ruthar is a vessel that chose. Malcanthet reveals some of this in section 1.

> [!readaloud] Back at the summit
> You're standing at the summit of the Reliquary again. The Shell is cold in your hand. The dust hangs in the air, not falling.
>
> And someone speaks, very close, just behind your shoulder.
>
> *"Oh, how perfect you are."*

## 1. Malcanthet

> [!info] DM Note
> Picks up the instant Session 54 ended. Malcanthet speaks with a **French accent**: lean into the rhythm and the little words (*mon cher, chéri, non, voilà, magnifique*), and don't spell the accent out phonetically.
> The scene is about **Wayne and his road to becoming the next Hollow Vessel**, with one turn to **Thimble** and his hunger for her Tome. No one else gets lines from her. The Godhand scene from Session 54 (Lolth, Alaric's card, Bog's "hay") is held for later.
> **Order:** her arrival, the admiration, what the memory meant, the offer, Thimble, the choice, then her parting jab at Aalyn, which brings Lolth (section 2). The Collapse slams back in after Lolth leaves.
> **She opened the cold open's memory for Wayne.** It's her pitch. She isn't hiding the choice, because she's certain he'll make the right one.
> She never lies. She leaves things out.

### Her arrival

> [!readaloud]
> The cracking has stopped. Not the danger, the *sound*. Dust hangs in the air in front of your faces. A chunk of the ceiling the size of a cart has broken free and simply stopped, halfway down, turning very slowly. The whole Reliquary is holding its breath.
>
> Wayne, the bead of void inside the Shell rises up the inside of the stone, like something pressing its face to a window. A thread of black pours out of it, over your fingers, down to the floor, and runs behind you.
>
> A hand, light as silk, rests on your shoulder.
>
> When she steps around you, she's tall, taller than any of you but Wayne. Black and liquid, shaped like a woman, great wings folded around her like a cloak. Her face is smooth and beautiful and has no features at all. Her voice comes from very close, as if she's speaking into your ear and no one else's.

### To Wayne: the admiration

- **(Circling him):** "Look at you, mon cher. Look at what you have done. They tried to make you still, my poor broken shadow, and you came back *harder*. Darker." *(She runs a finger down the ebony of his arm.)* "*Magnifique.*"
- **(The proof):** "You know how many have stood where you stand? Pilgrims, champions, little kings. They all became stone and stayed stone. You?" *(A delighted laugh.)* "You broke out of it. Not with fear. With a *memory*. Of an old man and a pot of soup." *(Softer.)* "Even I did not see that coming. I like to be surprised. It happens so rarely now."
- **(The diagnosis):** "You feel it, non? The stone, it does not hurt anymore. It fits you. You have stopped fighting your own skin. Your body is finished, chéri. It is a shell, a beautiful one." *(She taps his chest, where a heart would be.)* "But a cathedral with nothing inside is only a tomb."
- **(The two yeses):** "Twice now you have said yes to me. The needle waited for you, and you let it in. The blade asked you to let go, and you let go. Nobody has said yes to me twice since *him*."
- **(The regret):** "I left you unfinished, in the house where I made you. It is my one regret. It must ache, to be half of something."

- **(The memory):** "You remember now, non? Where we met." *(Delighted.)* "I gave it back to you. A gift."

### What the memory meant

- **(The field):** "That is where his world broke from yours. The giants fell back into their sky and the sky tore away behind them. And there, in the middle of it all, my masterpiece *turned*." *(Bitter, for one breath.)* "He chose wrong. He was weak at the end. All those voices inside him, his brothers, his cousins, whispering his name until he forgot whose he was."
- **(The choice, to Wayne):** "He gave you something, the old ghost. A little spark. I know. I saw. I let it happen." *(She tilts her head.)* "Because a vessel that cannot choose is only a tool. And I do not want a tool, mon cher. I want what he should have been. I want the one who chooses *me*."
- **(If Wayne asks about the armor, or the spark):** "Old things. Broken things. You can spend your life climbing after a dead man's leftovers." *(A shrug.)* "Or you can have *everything*."

### The offer

- **(The power):** "I am offering you the heart he lost. Take it into you, and I will give you power you do not even have the words to want. You will walk through walls like they are fog. You will walk through *armies* like they are fog."
- **(The shape of it, a little):** "A vessel is made in three parts, chéri. A body, a mind, a soul. The body, you have given me already. The rest, we will do together. Slowly. With love."
- **(The first step):** "Go to the west. To the mountain that shines and is not a mountain. My brothers and sisters are gathering there, and when the mountain opens, I need *you* standing in the wound." *(Fond.)* "You will know the place when you see it. The heart will pull you there."
- **(If Wayne asks what happens there):** "The world opens, a little. Like a door." *(She leaves it there.)*
- **(The brand):** *(She touches the mark on him, almost tenderly.)* "This ugly thing? It belongs to my brother, the one you robbed on his bridge. He has a long memory." *(A smile in her voice.)* "But the Five share everything now. Finish what we start, and he will give you to me, and I will burn it off you myself. No more mark. No more promise to anyone but me."

> [!secret] What she left out
> revealed:: false
> - "The heart will pull you there": the Shell was the Reliquary's anchor, the stitch that held an Abyssal house in the material plane. Planted in the wound at Glimmerstone, it stitches the Abyss to the world and holds the gateway open. **Wayne is the door.** See [[Become the Hollow Vessel]].
> - The brand comes off because a vessel needs no brand. It is already hers.

**If Wayne is defiant:** "Bien sûr. Be angry. He was angry too, at first." *(Patient.)* "I have waited five thousand years. I can wait for you to finish being angry."
**If Wayne is curious:** "Ask me anything, mon cher. I will tell you the truth. I always tell the truth." *(Beat.)* "It is the most effective lie there is."

### To Thimble

> [!info] DM Note
> She turns to Thimble before Wayne answers, to give Kevin time to think. Thimble has grown obsessed with her Tome and its power, and she can feel it. She loathes that he carries it, and she's unsettled by what he is. But his hunger is something she understands, and she plays it. She can't take the Tome. The binding won't let her. That doesn't stop her tempting him.

> [!readaloud]
> She lets go of Wayne, and turns her featureless face to Thimble.
>
> Something changes. The warmth goes out of the air all at once. Every surface of her liquid body bristles into fine black needles, the way the void did in the Mind trial when someone thought too hard, and then, slowly, they smooth back down.

- **(The Tome):** "And here is my little thief." *(Every word precise.)* "You have something of mine, petit voleur. A room of my own house, torn out and bound in ink." *(Closer.)* "It watches me, you know. Every hour I live, it writes me down."
- **(His obsession):** "You read it at night, when the others sleep. I feel your little fingers on my name. On my *true* name." *(Not angry. Interested.)* "You are hungry, non? Not for gold. Not for spells. For the thing *under* the words."
- **(What she felt in him):** "When your light went out in the trial, I reached for what was under it. To take it, the way I take everyone." *(A pause. For the first time she sounds uncertain.)* "There was nothing to hold. It *moved*. It smelled like the beginning. Like us, before we were anything at all." *(Very quiet.)* "What did he make you out of, little thief?"
- **(If Thimble asks who "he" is):** "Ask your father. He is the one who looked." *(That's all she'll give.)*
- **(If Thimble asks about the rifts, the planes, the murals):** *(Amused.)* "The walls between the worlds? Oh, chéri. Things like you and me were born before there were walls."
- **(The temptation):** "You want what is in that book? It is power, petit. The kind that does not ask permission. But a book is only a door." *(Her face tilts, studying him.)* "And you, I think, are already something on the other side of it."
- **(Home):** "You are going home soon, I think. The shining mountain. It will be so glad to see you." *(Softly.)* "It remembers what you are made of."
- **(The warning, or the invitation):** "Read it, then. Every page. Every page you read reads a little more of you. I cannot take it from you." *(Beat.)* "But when you are tired of reading, little thief, come and find me. I will teach you the parts the book is too afraid to say."

> [!secret] What this sets up
> revealed:: false
> Thimble is chaos-born, kin to the obyriths ([[The Chaos Primordial]]). She recognized it and it frightens her a little, which is why she flatters instead of threatens. The invitation is a seed for Thimble's own temptation in the Glimmerstone arc, and the "Ask your father" line points at his father's role there.

### The choice

> [!readaloud]
> She turns back to Wayne, last of all, and holds out her liquid hands, palms up, like someone offering to take a coat.
>
> "So. The heart, mon cher. Take it into you, or carry it in your pocket like a pretty stone." *(A small, indulgent shrug of her great wings.)* "I am very patient. But the house is falling, and I think your friends would like to leave."

**If Wayne accepts**

> [!readaloud]
> She takes the Shell from your hand, gently, the way you'd take something from a sleeping child. She presses it to your chest. Right there, where you put your hand through the Remnant's heart.
>
> It goes in.
>
> No pain. Your stone parts around it like water and closes over it like it was never open. Then a beat. A slow, heavy pulse, from somewhere deep inside you, that isn't yours. Under the ebony of your chest, for a moment, you can see the void move, a dark shape turning beneath the surface.
>
> And your body feels *light*. Lighter than it's ever been. As if walls and floors and stone were only suggestions.
>
> "*Voilà.*" She sounds like someone watching the first snow. "Until the dark, my love."

- Wayne gains **Shade Step** from the [[Shell of the Void Heart]], and the [[Become the Hollow Vessel]] questline begins. The Shell no longer uses an attunement slot. The curse and the Hollowing track apply from now on.
- Optional: Wayne can use his first Shade Step in the Collapse, straight through a falling wall.

**If Wayne refuses**

> [!readaloud]
> "Non?" She sounds almost pleased, the way a cat sounds pleased with a mouse that runs. "Keep it, then. Carry it. It is yours either way."
>
> The bead of void inside the Shell sinks down and goes still. The stone goes cool and grey and quiet in your hand, just a broken pendant, worn smooth by some long-dead climber's fingers.
>
> "When you are ready, it will wake. Until the dark, my love."

- The Shell goes **completely dormant**: no powers, no curse, no Hollowing. He can carry it. The offer stays open, and the questline doesn't start until he accepts.

### Her parting jab

> [!readaloud]
> As she turns to go, her featureless face passes over Aalyn, and lingers just long enough to be an insult.

- **(To Aalyn, dismissive):** "And Lolth's little songbird. She lets you out so far from the web." *(A soft laugh.)* "How trusting of her."

> [!readaloud]
> The liquid of her body collapses all at once, a held breath let go, and is gone.
>
> But the dust doesn't fall. The house is still holding its breath. And the air is getting cold.

## 2. Lolth

> [!info] DM Note
> Malcanthet's jab wakes her. Lolth is **furious** with Aalyn: in the Soul trial he used *her* bowstring to reach past her for Sumina, and she felt it pull. This scene is aggressive, belittling, and demeaning. She reminds him exactly how much power she has over him. **She can't act and can't be harmed.** She's a presence, not a body. Keep it to 10 or 15 minutes, all on Aalyn.
> Held for later: the Lolth and Malcanthet rivalry banter, and Alaric's Fenwyn moment and the wolf card (Session 54, section 3).

### How Lolth Talks

- **A chorus a half-beat behind her.** Her voice is one voice, but every few words a whisper of smaller voices repeats the last word or two after her, like a thousand spiders agreeing. Do it yourself by repeating the end of a line in a hush: "You used my string. *(my string.)*"
- **She draws out her s's and soft sounds,** and never rushes. She has all the time in the world, and she wants him to feel how little of it he has.
- **Clicks between sentences.** Tap a fingernail on the table, two quick taps, when she pauses. Mandibles.
- **She never says his name.** He's "songbird," "little bird," "pet," "thing." Once, at her coldest, "it."
- **She repeats his own words back at him,** slowly, as if they're stupid. She asks questions and answers them herself before he can.
- **Old court manners gone rotten.** She was a queen of the fey before she was a spider. She's formal, precise, and cruel the way an aristocrat is cruel to a servant.
- **Her threats sound like gifts,** and her gifts sound like threats.
- **Physically,** she moves wrong: her head tilts at angles a neck shouldn't allow, her fingers are always weaving thread, and she stands too close.

### Her Arrival

> [!readaloud]
> It's snowing.
>
> Not ash. Snow, fine and cold, falling out of nowhere onto the black stone of the summit. A winter cold comes with it that doesn't belong in this place. And out of every crack in the floor and every shadow on the walls, threads begin to spin: silver, thin as hair, running across the chamber in long lines until the whole summit is strung like the inside of a web.
>
> Aalyn, something tightens around your neck. You can't see it. You can feel it.
>
> She steps out from between the threads as if they were a curtain. Tall, drow, beautiful in a way that makes you want to look away, white hair pinned with tiny pale spiders that move when she does. You can see the snow falling through her. It doesn't matter.
>
> Her head tilts. Too far.
>
> "*Trusting.*" She tastes the word. (*...trusting.*) "Is that what she calls it? I call it a leash long enough to hang himself with." She looks at Aalyn, and smiles. "*Again.*"

> [!info] DM Note
> "Again" is a knife only you understand: Aalyn hanged himself from the winter tree, and Lolth caught him. He doesn't remember. Don't explain it.

### To Aalyn

**The fury**
- "You used my *string*." *(She reaches out and touches the golden bowstring, and its light dims under her finger.)* "The gift I put in your hand. You used it to reach *past* me, for something of *mine*." *(Click, click.)* "Did you think I wouldn't feel it pull? Did you think I was *asleep*?"
- "I felt every inch of it, songbird. Every note. You plucked my web like a child plucking a harp, and you thought you were *clever*."

**Belittling him**
- "Look at you. Covered in another woman's gold." *(The gold cloak.)* "A little bird in borrowed feathers, singing in a dead giant's house. Is that what you are now?"
- "You were a *wreck* when I found you, pet. A boy in the snow with nothing left. Everything you are, I wove. Your voice. Your luck. Your *breath*." (*...your breath.*)
- "'*Make the world a better place.*'" *(His own promise, repeated slowly, mocking.)* "And how is that going? A dead princess. A city in mist. A brand you can't read." *(Beat.)* "Better for whom, little bird?"

**Sumina**
- "She is not yours. She was never yours." *(Very close.)* "You think you lost a girl from your village under a winter tree." *(Her head tilts.)* "You have *no idea* what you lost. And you never will, unless I decide you may."

**Her power over him** *(the thread tightens; no damage, real pain)*
> [!readaloud]

## 3. The Collapse

> [!info] DM Note
> Adapted from [[Session 54 - The Heart of the Void]], section 5. The party has to climb back down (which is up) through every floor: the Summit, Soul, Mind, Body, the Turning, and the Nave. **Six stages.** Atul joins at Stage 2, and **whether he gets out is at stake.**

> [!readaloud]
> The void below the rim of the summit begins to turn, slowly, like water circling a drain. The wind rises. The heartbeat starts again, faster, faster, until it isn't a heartbeat anymore. It's a drum.
>
> The house is going home. And you are in it.

### How It Works

**The Collapse Clock: 8 boxes.**
- Each stage fills **1 box** no matter what. A failed stage fills **1 extra box**.
- The party can afford **two failures** and still walk out on their own.

**Each stage**
1. Read the stage.
2. Each player describes how their character gets through and makes the named check, or any check they can justify. **DC 14.**
3. **Group check:** if at least half succeed, the stage succeeds.
4. **Each individual failure** takes the stage's cost either way.

**Running notes**
- One pass around the table per stage. Keep it fast. Helping grants advantage. A great idea can be an automatic success.
- **Wayne's Shade Step** *(only if he accepted the Shell)*: an automatic success on a stage for himself, and he can carry one ally through inside his shadow. 3 charges. Each use once they're gone gives him **1 Hollowing**. Make the temptation clear without pushing. If he refused the Shell, it's a dead grey stone in his pocket and does nothing.

### Stage 1: The Summit Falls

> [!readaloud]
> The calcified branches snap and fall away into the turning dark. The floor of the summit is coming apart in rings, from the edge inward, each ring tipping and sliding down into the whirlpool below. The way down to the Soul chamber is a ring that's already starting to tilt.

- **Check:** Athletics or Acrobatics.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage.

### Stage 2: The Overflowing Well, and the King

> [!readaloud]
> The Chamber of Soul is flooding. The Sea of Void pours out of the shaft and across the floor, rising into needles and reaching hands. The great sword is still hanging in its chains, and the chains are snapping one by one.
>
> And standing at the edge of the well, exactly where you left him, is King Atul. He turns at the sound of you, and something in his stone face eases.
>
> "There you are. I was starting to think I'd have to come find you. Which I can't." He glances at the void, then at you, and his eyes narrow. "Two of you came down darker than you went up. We'll talk about that later. Move."

- **Check:** Dexterity saving throw, or Wisdom saving throw to hold steady.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d8 psychic damage.
- **Wayne, Thimble, and Bog** pass untouched. The void doesn't notice them. Let that be unsettling.
- **Atul** is a mind with no soul, so the void ignores him too. He can carry one ally through on his shoulder, automatically.

**Atul for the rest of the Collapse**
- Once per stage, he can give **one character an automatic success** by hauling, shielding, or throwing them. He's a stone dwarf king; make it look like it.
- He talks the whole way, short and gruff: *"Left."* *"Mind the edge."* *"I've held worse than this. I held a wedding."*

### Stage 3: The Cracked Sphere

> [!readaloud]
> In the Chamber of Mind, the great pale sphere has split along the crack your needles made, and it's bleeding light. Where the light spills across the floor, the floor stops being real: pale tiles from the mindscape, floating over the drop down the shaft. You can't tell which tiles are stone.
>
> But you could tell before. For each other.

- **Same guide loop as the Mind trial.** Each guide calls a path for their walker, live and fast.
- **Check:** the guide makes Insight or Investigation, the walker makes Acrobatics. A pair succeeds only if both do. *(Count pairs; the group still needs half.)*
- **Alaric** can clash one illusion for an automatic success on one pair.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage, and 1d4 psychic for anyone who talks in the mindscape.

### Stage 4: The Shells Remember

> [!readaloud]
> In the Chamber of Body, the walls are rippling again. Your shadows are stepping out of them, half-formed this time, cracked and crumbling, still hollow-eyed. They don't attack. They *reach*, each one for its own original, grabbing arms and ankles to drag you back into the stone.

- **Check:** Athletics or Acrobatics to break free.
- **The trick still works:** freeing someone else's grip instead of your own is an automatic success for both.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage as the stone grinds at you.
- **Wayne's shadow** wears no pendant. Its throat is bare. If Wayne took the Shell into his chest, it reaches for his chest. If he refused, it reaches for his pocket.

### Stage 5: The Turning, Turning

> [!readaloud]
> On the great stair, gravity can't decide which way is down. It flips, and flips again, every few heartbeats. The stair is a wall, then a ceiling, then a floor. Far above, the pale circle of the shaft swings back and forth like a lantern on a chain.

- **Check:** Athletics, Acrobatics, or Perception to time the flips.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage.
- **Bogtrotter** can run the buttresses and catch one falling ally with no roll.
- **Atul is heavy.** If anyone fails, Atul fell with them. Someone has to spend their turn hauling him back up (**Athletics DC 15**), or he loses his auto-success at the doors and **Stage 6 gets harder for him** (see below).

### Stage 6: The Closing Doors

> [!readaloud]
> You come through the Turning and land on the floor of the nave, and the nave is tilting. Offering basins slide and shatter. The stone book slams shut on its lectern. Across the hall, the great doors are swinging closed on their own, slow and heavy, and the split heart carved where they meet is bleeding fresh.
>
> *"Pilgrim, give."*
>
> Atul doesn't slow down. He hits the doors shoulder-first, plants his feet, and stops them. The stone of his arms groans. Dust pours off him.
>
> "*Go,*" he says. "I've held worse."

- **Atul holds the doors.** Everyone gets through on a single **Dexterity (Acrobatics) DC 12** check each. Failure costs 2d6 bludgeoning (dragged through the gap).
- **The Offering Hall:** each character who succeeds can grab one item from the basins on the way past (use the Reliquary's offering items, or roll on the Bronze Vault table in section 10).

### Does Atul Get Out?

Check the Clock when the party is through the doors.
- **6 or fewer boxes filled:** Atul comes out last, step by grinding step, and the doors slam behind him. **He survives.**
- **7 boxes filled, or he fell on the Turning and nobody hauled him up:** he's stuck in the gap, holding the doors with everything he has. The party can save him with one last **group Athletics check DC 15** (pulling him through). Success: he tumbles out as the doors slam. Failure: see *If Atul Is Lost*.
- **8 boxes filled:** the doors start to close on all of them. See *If the Clock Fills*.

### If the Clock Fills

> [!readaloud]
> Through the black stone comes a roar of fire. The doors blow inward in a sheet of white flame. Standing in it, wreathed in fire so hot it's blue, are the fire sages of Vistro. They've broken their ring for the first time in an age. "*Out!*" one screams. "*OUT!*"

- Each character gains **1 Exhaustion**.
- **One fire sage doesn't make it out.** He holds the doors for the last of them and is dragged down with the house. Vistro will know his name.
- Atul is lost unless the party has an automatic success left to spend on him.

### If Atul Is Lost

> [!readaloud]
> The doors are closing on him, and he knows it. Atul looks at you through the narrowing gap, and he isn't afraid. He looks almost relieved.
>
> With a crack like a dropped plate, he snaps the last two fingers off his own stone hand and throws them through the gap. They skitter across the black glass and stop at your feet. The signet ring of House Stonemarch is still on one of them.
>
> "Those are Stonemarch hands. Down below, past the guild hall, there's a vault of dark stone. It opens to my hands and nothing else." The doors grind. "Tell my soldiers I'm sorry I led them across the lake."
>
> The doors close.

- The party gains **[[Atul's Stone Fingers]]** (the Dark Stone Vault key).
- Atul goes down with the Reliquary into the Abyss. Whether that's the end of him is your call. *(Malcanthet now has a dwarf king's stone body in her house. Vashara will hear of it.)*

## 8. The Soulvite Forge

> [!readaloud]
> The forge is a cathedral of anvils. A dozen of them stand in a ring around a central hearth the size of a house, each with its own hammer hanging on a chain above it, and over the hearth hangs a great bell-shaped hood of silver and iron. Above the hearth, three empty crystal cradles hang in a triangle, and around them, on three iron platforms, stand six control panels, two facing each cradle, their dials dark and their levers locked.
>
> Behind the hearth, half-sunk into the floor like it's sleeping, is a hulking shape of iron and brass, pipes running along its arms, a dark round socket in its chest.
>
> Around the walls, racks of half-finished work: blade blanks, breastplates, a dwarf-sized helm with no face. Everything in the room is waiting.

### How the Forge Works

- **Three containment orbs** power the hearth: **Silver**, **Ley**, and **Element**. Each sits in its own cradle above the hearth.
- **Each orb has two control panels:** a **Channeling panel**, which draws energy into the orb, and a **Containment panel**, which holds it there. Six panels, two people per orb.
- **The orbs:**
  - **Silver orb:** the Silver Foundry (the crucible cradle).
  - **Ley orb:** the Merchant's Alliance (the guildmaster's seat).
  - **Element orb:** inside the **Forge Defender**, glowing in its chest. *(The party only learns this when it wakes.)*
- **The ratio is set by the orbs.** How high the Silver and Ley orbs are charged sets the forge's ratio, and that ratio decides what it makes (see *Clausius or Vashara*).

### Waking the Defender

When the first orb is set in its cradle, the hearth flickers, and the shape behind it wakes.

> [!readaloud]
> Steam screams out of every pipe on its body at once. The hulking shape pulls itself up out of the floor: a dwarven war-construct twelve feet tall, iron and brass and silver, steam pouring from its joints like breath. The socket in its chest isn't empty. A crystal orb sits in it, full of shifting fire and water and wind and stone, all four at once. It turns toward the hearth, toward the orb you just set, and its voice comes out of a brass grille, flat and dwarven:
>
> "*UNAUTHORIZED RATIO. FORGE SECURED.*"

**The Soulvite Forge Defender (steam golem)**
- **Statblock:** a **Stone Golem** (Monster Manual, CR 10), re-flavored as iron and brass.
- **Steam Slam:** its Slam deals an extra **2d6 fire** damage.
- **Scalding Vent (Recharge 5–6):** replaces Slow. A 30-foot cone of superheated steam. **DC 17 Constitution save**, 6d8 fire damage on a failure, half on a success.
- **Immune to fire.** When it takes cold damage, its speed is halved until the end of its next turn as its steam condenses.
- **The Element orb:** it draws power from the orb in its chest. While anyone is working a Channeling panel, the Defender regains **10 HP** at the start of its turn (it's drinking from the forge). Get the orb out to stop it.
  - **Prying it out:** once the Defender is below half HP, a creature adjacent to it can use an action to grab the orb: **Athletics DC 18** (Sleight of Hand DC 18 works too). Success: the orb comes free and the Defender loses its regeneration and its Scalding Vent.
  - **Killing it** also frees the orb.

> [!info] DM Note
> The fun version: some players fight the Defender while others start working the panels, knowing every turn on a Channeling panel heals it. Or the party shuts everything down, kills it first, then lights the forge in peace. Let them choose.

### Lighting the Forge: the Six Panels

Once all three orbs are in their cradles, two characters work each orb's panels. Run it in initiative order.

**The Charge track.** Each orb has a Charge from **0 to 6**. It starts at 0. The goal is to bring each orb to its **target** and **lock** it there.

**The targets** *(from the calibration chart in the Foundry, or from Atul)*

| Ratio | Silver orb | Ley orb | Element orb |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Clausius (3:1)** | 6 | 2 | 4 |
| **Vashara (1:1)** | 4 | 4 | 4 |

- **Without the chart or Atul:** **Arcana DC 16** or **Smith's Tools DC 15** to work out safe targets by reading the dials. On a failure, the party guesses, and you choose what the forge makes.

**On each Channeling panel turn**
- Choose how hard to push, then roll **Arcana** or **Smith's Tools**: **+1 Charge (DC 12)**, **+2 (DC 14)**, or **+3 (DC 16)**.
- Failure: no Charge gained.

**On each Containment panel turn**
- Roll **Constitution save, Arcana, or Tinker's Tools**, **DC 10 + the orb's current Charge**.
- Success: the Charge holds. If the orb is at its target, it **locks** (no more checks needed for that orb).
- Failure: **backlash.** Both of that orb's operators take 2d6 damage (Silver: force; Ley: psychic; Element: fire), and the orb loses 1 Charge.

**Overload.** If an orb goes above 6, it **cracks**: a 10-foot burst of 4d6 force around its cradle (Dex save DC 15 for half), and its Charge resets to 0. A second crack **shatters** the orb, and the forge can only ever be run at one ratio, locked to whatever the other two orbs are set to. *(Or it's a reason to go find another orb.)*

**Not enough hands.** Five players and six panels: someone runs two panels (one check per turn, alternating), or a helper takes one.
- **Atul (if he survived):** he runs any one panel with **advantage**, and his Containment checks automatically succeed. He also knows both ratios by heart and what each one means.
  - "Three to one, Clapeyron's way. It makes good steel that doesn't think." *(Pause.)* "One to one is my wife's way. It makes steel that loves you back." *(He doesn't say which he'd choose.)*
  - If the party is losing control: "Ease off the silver! You're feeding it faster than it can hold!"
- **A salamander (Vistro's labor boon):** works one Channeling panel at **+5**.

**When all three orbs are locked**

> [!readaloud]
> The three orbs blaze at once. Lines of light run down the chains into the hearth, and the hearth catches with a sound like a held breath let go: white fire, then blue, then a color with no name at all. Every hammer on every chain starts swinging by itself, slow and steady, a heartbeat you can feel in your teeth.
>
> The Soulvite Forge is awake.

### Clausius or Vashara

The ratio the party locked decides how the forge works, every time, until someone relights it.

**Clausius's ratio (3:1): stable.** Items come out clean and reliable.

**Vashara's ratio (1:1): the metal remembers.** Items come out **one step stronger**. But each one is a little bit *aware*. It warms to its wielder, hums when they're in danger, and hates being put down. You may give it one mild quirk from the **Sentient Items** rules (it has opinions). **Insight DC 13 (holding a 1:1 item for the first time):** it feels like being *watched kindly*. Like the Shell.

### Crafting

- **1 hour of work** at the forge and a **Smith's Tools DC 15** check. On a failure, the material isn't lost; try again in an hour.
- **Materials:** **Soulvite Ingots** (Foundry, Silver Vault, or cast with the mold) and **minor Soulvite shards**. **The four boss shards are not crafting material.** They're needed at the Confluence.
- **The Soulvite ingot mold:** with the forge lit, 4 hours and 200 gp of silver casts **1 Soulvite Ingot** (Smith's Tools DC 14). Clausius's chart warns the ley line can't feed more than a few: **3 castings at most** before the forge needs a rest.

| Materials | Clausius (3:1) | Vashara (1:1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Ingot | **+1 Weapon**, **+1 Armor**, or **+1 Shield** | **+2 Weapon** or **+2 Shield** (with a quirk) |
| 1 Ingot + 1 minor shard | **Armor of Resistance** (the shard's element) or a **+1 Weapon** that deals +1d4 of the shard's element | The same, plus the wielder senses hostile creatures of that element within 60 ft (with a quirk) |
| 2 Ingots | **+2 Weapon** or **+2 Armor** | **+3 Weapon** (with a quirk); only once, ever, and the forge cracks after |

- All re-flavored official items. In D&D Beyond, build them from the official item and add the Soulvite flavor text.

### Discoveries

- **The faceless helm** on the rack: dwarven work, unfinished, with a socket inside the brow exactly the shape of the Mind Medallion's stone. **Investigation DC 14:** a note in Vashara's hand tucked inside it. *"For A. If I can't wake him, I'll make him a better face."*
- **The order ledger:** the forge's last commission, signed by the Merchant Alliance and countersigned by the Crown: *"Forty blades, one to one, delivery by the Darkening."* It was never delivered.
