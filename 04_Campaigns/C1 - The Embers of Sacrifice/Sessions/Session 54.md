## 0. What the Blade Choice Means

> [!secret] Wayne and Thimble, the sheathed
> revealed:: false
> When a light goes out in the Soul trial, the soul isn't destroyed. It's **sheathed**: folded down small and dark, the way the Vessel's was, so the void stops seeing it as prey. That's why the void let them pass. It's also why their brands darkened. A darkened brand is a claim that has started to close.

**What changes, permanently**
- **Darkened brands.** Black now, not red. They don't burn in dark places anymore. They go cold, and that's worse.
- **Malcanthet knows their names.** She can find them, though she can't reach them without a tether (Wayne has one through the Void Trial's mark; Thimble doesn't, which bothers her).
- **Two blades from one forge.** Their darkened brands resonate. Wayne's psionic link to Thimble, the one the needles broke in the Mind trial, now just works.
  - **Mechanic:** Wayne's *Psychic Whispers* link to Thimble is permanent, has unlimited range on the same plane, and costs no Psionic Energy die.
  - **Cost:** each feels a cold jolt when the other drops to 0 HP. And when either is near the Abyss, both brands ache.
- **The void ignores them** in places where the Abyss leaks through. Hollowed things, Void Shades, and the Sea of Void pass over them unless they're attacked first. This lasts for the rest of the Reliquary, and returns anywhere the Abyss is thin.
- The Session 53 perks (advantage against charm and fear, +1d6 necrotic on weapon attacks) last until they leave the Reliquary.

**How Malcanthet feels about each of them**
- **Wayne:** delighted. He said yes twice: once when the needle waited for him in the Mind trial, and once when he became the blade. She takes it as courtship.
- **Thimble:** it's complicated now. She already hated the Tome thief. Then his light went out, and the void reached underneath it and found **raw chaos**, every color at once, something that can't be sheathed or hollowed. She doesn't know what he is. She's never not known something before.

**What it means between them**
- Wayne is stone: stillness. Thimble is chaos underneath. They're the two halves of what the Lattice and the lithoweaves were built around, standing side by side with matching black brands. Nobody needs to say that yet. Let them notice the brands match.

## 2. The Hollow Vessel Remnant

> [!info] DM Note
> The Remnant isn't the Vessel. It's the Shell's immune system: the heart's last defense, built from the darkened soul the party fed it in the Soul trial. It fights the way the trials taught. Every mechanic below is a lesson the party already learned, used against them or for them.

**The Summit**
- Round chamber, about 60 ft across. Open void below the rim in every direction.
- **The Heart** sits in the center on a pedestal drowned in void, beating. Calcified branches grow out of it like a dead tree.
- **Three Veins**, thick ropes of calcified void, run from the heart to three anchors around the chamber, about 25 ft out, evenly spaced. The Remnant draws on them.
- **Five calcified branches** reach across the floor between the anchors. They give half cover in Phase 1 and become the only safe footing in Phase 2.

> [!readaloud] The Remnant
> He fills the chamber the way a storm fills a sky. Grey stone plates hang off a body of liquid black, a crown of stone sits on a head with no face under it, and three thick veins of calcified void run from his back to the heart behind him, pulsing with every beat. The blade in his hand is the same black stone as the sword you rode down into the dark.
>
> He looks over all of you without seeing anyone at all.
>
> Then he looks at Wayne. And he sees him.

### The Veins

All three must be severed before the Remnant can be truly hurt. Each is an object: **AC 15, 25 HP, immune to poison and psychic damage**. Each one tests one of the trials.

**The Vein of Body** *(grey stone, carved with faces)*
- A creature can't damage it if the Remnant attacked or targeted that creature since the end of the Remnant's last turn. The stone remembers who it's fighting.
- **The lesson:** the Body trial. Swap partners. Whoever the Remnant is busy with holds its attention while someone else cuts.

**The Vein of Mind** *(pale, bristling with needles)*
- It's **invisible to whoever is attacking it**, so attacks against it have disadvantage.
- An ally who can see the Vein can use their **Reaction** to call the strike ("left, now"), turning that disadvantage into advantage instead.
- **Alaric's illusion clash** works here too. If he casts an illusion onto it, it flickers into view for everyone until the start of his next turn.
- **Words taken in the Mind trial are still gone.** Calling a strike with a forbidden word costs 1d4 psychic damage, same as before.
- **The lesson:** the Mind trial. Trust the voice that leads you, as long as it's a friend's.

**The Vein of Soul** *(black, wet, beating with the heart)*
- Spells and ranged attacks can't harm it. It has to be struck by hand or by a held weapon.
- A creature **whose light still burns** takes **9 (2d8) necrotic dam age** each time it hits the Vein.
- **Wayne and Thimble** can instead use their action to grab it and pull, dealing **16 (3d10)** damage to it with no roll and taking nothing. The void doesn't see them as alive anymore.
- **Bogtrotter** has no brand, and in the Soul trial he found he could stand on the void. The Vein doesn't burn him either. Don't explain why.
- **The lesson:** the Soul trial. What the blade-chosen gave up is now the key.

**When all three are severed**
> [!readaloud]
> The last vein snaps with a sound like a mast breaking. Black ichor sprays from the stumps. The Remnant staggers, and for the first time, its blade drops to the floor and it drops to one knee, the heart behind it stuttering, missing a beat.

- The Remnant loses its **Tethered** resistance and is **Stunned until the end of its next turn**. That's the damage window. Tell the table it's open.

### Phase 2: The Void Unbound

When the Remnant drops to 0 HP, it doesn't die.

> [!readaloud]
> The stone plates shatter all at once and rain across the floor, and what's underneath doesn't fall. It rises. Pure void, black and slick, a giant shaped from oil and nothing, with a hole where its chest should be and the heart's beat booming out of it.
>
> And the floor goes. The void pours up over the rim of the chamber from every side and floods the summit ankle-deep, black and glassy, bristling into needles wherever someone stands. Only the calcified branches stay above it.

**The Void Floor**
- The floor is now the Sea of Void. Only the **five calcified branches** are safe footing (each about 10 ft by 20 ft).
- A creature **whose light still burns** that ends its turn standing on the void takes **7 (2d6) necrotic damage**.
- **Wayne, Thimble, and Bog** walk on it freely.
- **Branches break.** Each time the Remnant uses **Sweep**, it can shatter a branch within reach instead of attacking. Anyone on it drops onto the void. The safe ground keeps shrinking, so where people stand starts to matter.

**The Heartbeat (initiative count 20, losing ties)**
> [!readaloud]
> The heart beats, and the void hears it.

- Spikes of void lance up toward the creature that **cast a spell or concentrated** most recently. **DC 15 Dexterity saving throw**, **13 (3d8) necrotic damage** on a failure, half on a success. If no one has cast since the last count 20, the spikes go for whoever is closest to the heart.
- This one hits the blade-chosen too. The heart isn't the void floor. It's the Abyss itself, and it's paying attention.

## 4. The Shell and Wayne's Memory

**The choice is the table's.** No one has to take it. If no one does, the pedestal cracks beneath it after a minute, the Shell drops through into the dark, and the house goes home anyway. ## 5. The Collapse

> [!readaloud] The moment the Shell leaves the pedestal
> Nothing happens. For one long breath, nothing at all.
>
> Then the void below the rim of the summit begins to turn, slowly, like water circling a drain. The wind rises. The heartbeat starts again, faster, faster, until it isn't a heartbeat anymore. It's a drum.
>
> The house is going home. And you are in it.

### How It Works

Climb back down (which is up) through every floor: the Summit, Soul, Mind, Body, the Turning, and the Nave. **Six stages.**

**The Collapse Clock: 8 boxes.**
- Each stage fills **1 box** no matter what. A failed stage fills **1 extra box**.
- The party can afford **two failures** and still walk out the doors on their own.

**Each stage**
1. Read the stage.
2. Each player describes how their character gets through and makes the named check, or any check they can justify. **DC 14.**
3. **Group check:** if at least half succeed, the stage succeeds.
4. **Each individual failure** takes the stage's cost either way.

**Running notes**
- One pass around the table per stage. Keep it fast.
- Helping grants advantage. A great idea can be an automatic success.
- **Wayne's Shade Step** auto-succeeds a stage for himself and can carry one ally inside his shadow. The Shell has 3 charges. Each use once they're gone gives him **1 Hollowing**. Make the temptation clear without pushing.
- **Atul** joins at Stage 2 (see below).

### Stage 1: The Summit Falls

> [!readaloud]
> The calcified branches snap and fall away into the turning dark. The floor of the summit is coming apart in rings, from the edge inward, each ring tipping and sliding down into the whirlpool below. The way down to the Soul chamber is a ring that's already starting to tilt.

- **Check:** Athletics or Acrobatics.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage.

### Stage 2: The Overflowing Well, and the King

> [!readaloud]
> The Chamber of Soul is flooding. The Sea of Void pours out of the shaft and across the floor, rising into needles and reaching hands. The great sword is still hanging in its chains, and the chains are snapping one by one.
>
> And standing at the edge of the well, exactly where you left him, is King Atul. He turns at the sound of you, and something in his stone face eases.
>
> "There you are. I was starting to think I'd have to come find you. Which I can't." He glances at the void, then at you, and his eyes narrow. "Two of you came down darker than you went up. We'll talk about that later. Move."

- **Check:** Dexterity saving throw, or Wisdom saving throw to hold steady.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d8 psychic damage.
- **Wayne, Thimble, and Bog** pass untouched. The void doesn't notice them. Let that be unsettling.
- **Atul** is a mind with no soul. The void ignores him too. He can carry one ally through on his shoulder, automatically.

**Atul for the rest of the Collapse**
- Once per stage, he can give **one character an automatic success** by hauling, shielding, or throwing them. He's a stone dwarf king; it should look like it.
- He talks the whole way, short and gruff: *"Left."* *"Mind the edge."* *"I've held worse than this. I held a wedding."*

### Stage 3: The Cracked Sphere

> [!readaloud]
> In the Chamber of Mind, the great pale sphere has split along the crack your needles made, and it's bleeding light. Where the light spills across the floor, the floor stops being real: pale tiles from the mindscape, floating over the drop down the shaft. You can't tell which tiles are stone.
>
> But you could tell before. For each other.

- **Same guide loop as the Mind trial.** Each guide calls a path for their walker, live, fast.
- **Check:** guide makes Insight or Investigation, walker makes Acrobatics. A pair succeeds only if both do. *(Count five pairs; the group still needs three.)*
- **Alaric** can clash one illusion for an automatic success on one pair.
- **Words taken in the Mind trial are still gone.** 1d4 psychic per slip.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage.

### Stage 4: The Shells Remember

> [!readaloud]
> In the Chamber of Body, the walls are rippling again. Your shadows are stepping out of them, half-formed this time, cracked and crumbling, still hollow-eyed. They don't attack. They *reach*, each one for its own original, grabbing arms and ankles to drag you back into the stone.

- **Check:** Athletics or Acrobatics to break free.
- **The trick still works:** freeing someone else's grip instead of your own is an automatic success for both. If the party remembers, the whole stage can succeed.
- **Cost of failure:** 1 Stone (the Pull track).
- **Wayne's shadow** wears no pendant this time. Its throat is bare. It reaches for the hand holding the Shell.

### Stage 5: The Turning, Turning

> [!readaloud]
> On the great stair, gravity can't decide which way is down. It flips, and flips again, every few heartbeats. The stair is a wall, then a ceiling, then a floor. Far above, the pale circle of the shaft swings back and forth like a lantern on a chain.

- **Check:** Athletics, Acrobatics, or Perception to time the flips.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage.
- **Bogtrotter** can run the buttresses and catch one falling ally with no roll.
- **Atul is heavy.** If anyone fails, Atul fell with them. Someone has to help haul him up, or he loses his auto-success on the last stage.

### Stage 6: The Closing Doors

> [!readaloud]
> You come through the Turning and land on the floor of the nave, and the nave is tilting. Offering basins slide and shatter. The stone book slams shut on its lectern. Across the hall, the great doors are swinging closed on their own, slow and heavy, and the split heart carved where they meet is bleeding fresh.
>
> *"Pilgrim, give."*
>
> Atul doesn't slow down. He hits the doors shoulder-first, plants his feet, and stops them. The stone of his arms groans. Dust pours off him.
>
> "*Go,*" he says. "I've held worse."

- **Atul holds the doors.** Everyone gets through on a single **Dexterity (Acrobatics) DC 12** check each. Failure costs 2d6 bludgeoning (dragged through the gap).
- **The Offering Hall:** each character who succeeds can grab one item from the basins on the way past.
- **Atul comes out last**, step by grinding step, and the doors slam behind him.

### If the Clock Fills

If the eighth box fills first, the doors start to close on all of them, Atul included.

> [!readaloud]
> Then, through the black stone, comes a roar of fire. The doors blow inward in a sheet of white flame. Standing in it, wreathed in fire so hot it's blue, are the fire sages of Vistro. They've broken their ring for the first time in an age. "*Out!*" one screams. "*OUT!*"

- Each character gains **1 Exhaustion**.
- **One fire sage doesn't make it out.** He holds the doors for the last of them and is dragged down with the house. Vistro will know his name.

### How to Save Atul

The party "saves" him if the medallion ends up back in their hands, or on his brow, before it goes dark. Ways that work:
- **Pay the price.** Hand over the Tome, or swear the oath. She keeps her word, sets the medallion back on his forehead herself, and lingers one long moment with her hand there.
- **Take it back.** It's in her hand, and she's watching all of them. **Sleight of Hand contested by her Perception (+7)**, or a grab (**Athletics contested by her Athletics (+3)**, with advantage if she's distracted). Alaric's illusions, Bog's speed, a well-timed distraction from anyone: reward a plan.
- **Persuade her.** **Persuasion DC 18**, or DC 15 if someone uses what Atul said to her in the Collapse, or what he's saying now. She doesn't want to be the one who stilled him twice.
- **Let Atul do it.** If someone gives Atul a reason, he can use his last strength to do one thing. If they ask him to grab her wrist, he does. Then it's a contest she can't win: his stone grip against her, **automatic**. But it costs him: the strain cracks him, and he loses **2 exchanges** on the spot.

**If anyone attacks her**
- *(Her eyes go white.)* She petrifies the attacker's weapon arm instantly, no roll; it reverts at the end of the scene. "That was a warning. You've seen what I do when I stop warning."
- If attacked again, she takes Atul and goes (see below).

## 8. The Lava Floor Sandbox

> [!info] DM Note: how to run it
> The party lands at the **Lava Docks**, the hub. From there they can go anywhere in any order. Nothing here is required tonight. Realistically, expect **one or two areas** in the second half of the session. The rest carries over.

**Hub and spokes**

| Area | What happens there | Needs power? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Lava Docks** (hub) | Landing, Atul's army on the shore, the dead elevator | No |
| **The Silver Foundry** | Combat, journals, loot, the calibration notes | No |
| **The Power Station** | Flame Warden and Stone Warden, the fire and earth shards | It's the power |

**Lava floor rules**
- **Heat.** Without fire resistance, each hour spent on the lava floor outside the Docks or the Alliance: **DC 12 Constitution saving throw** or 1 Exhaustion. *Vistro's boon of safe passage cancels this.*

### The Silver Foundry

> [!readaloud]
> Heat rolls out of the foundry before you see it. The hall is long and low, built around a smelting apparatus three stories tall: a great crucible of blackened iron hanging over a lava trough, feeding molten silver down a cascade of silver-lined channels. The silver is still moving. A thousand years untended, and it's still moving, slow and bright, and as it flows it isn't quite silver. Threads of color slide through it: blue, gold, green, colors that don't belong in metal.
>
> At the far end, under a row of hooded lamps, stand the quality-control stations, their benches still covered in papers.

**Points of interest**
- **The Smelting Apparatus.** The heart of the "how." **Arcana DC 14:** the silver is being fed raw elemental energy from four conduits (air, water, fire, earth), and something else, a fifth feed from the floor itself: ley energy from the Runelands. The four elementals were the catalysts. The ley line is what made it Soulvite.
- **The Cooling Channels.** A maze of silver troughs, steam venting where they cross. **Dex save DC 12** to cross at speed or take 2d6 fire. They can be **opened and closed** from three valve wheels (useful in the fight below).
- **The QC Stations.** Two sets of papers, side by side (see *Journals*).

**The fight: Batch 32**

Investigating the QC stations triggers it. The failed batches never cooled. They've been waiting.

> [!readaloud]
> The silver in the nearest channel stops flowing. It bunches. It rises, dripping, into a shape like a hound made of mercury, colors sliding under its skin, and the channels on either side of it begin to rise too.

- **1 Slag Mass:** a **Black Pudding** (Monster Manual), re-flavored as a pool of failed Soulvite. Instead of acid, its damage is **fire**, and it corrodes nonmagical metal the same way.
- **3 Dwarven Spheres** and **4 Dwarven Spiders** (your Durnhollow construct statblocks), re-skinned as malformed Soulvite: their silver is wrong-colored and soft.
- **Unstable colors.** At the start of each round, roll 1d4 for all the Soulvite enemies: **1 fire, 2 cold, 3 lightning, 4 thunder.** That round, their attacks deal that damage type instead of their normal type, and they're **resistant** to it. Players who notice (**Insight or Arcana DC 12**) can play around it.
- **Quench it.** Opening a valve wheel (an action, **Athletics DC 13**) floods a 20-ft section of channel with superheated steam. Any Soulvite creature in it is **Restrained** until the end of its next turn as its silver seizes, and takes 2d6 fire. Anyone else there takes the 2d6 fire too.
- **Lava surge** (optional, round 4): the trough overflows; low channels become difficult terrain and deal 2d6 fire to anyone ending a turn in them.

### The Power Station: Flame Warden and Stone Warden

> [!info] DM Note
> The September design is locked: tag-team boss, Scorching Charge and Stone Hide, no extra hazard. This adds the read-aloud, the stat basis, and what "power on" does.

> [!readaloud]
> The power station is a hexagonal cavern eighty feet across, its floor broken into islands of rock over streams of open lava. Four great heating cores rise out of the lava like chimneys, venting heat up through shafts in the ceiling to the floors above. Around the edges, three silver rods stand cold, and on the high ledges, two dwarven ballistas sit with their bolts still racked.
>
> In the center, two shapes are circling each other, the way they have for a thousand years: one of moving fire, one of grinding stone. When you step onto the first island, they both stop. And turn.

**Statblocks** *(Monster Manual, re-flavored)*
- **Flame Warden:** a **Fire Elemental**, with HP raised to 150 and Legendary Actions (2/round: move up to its speed, or one Touch attack). Climbs and perches on the heating cores.
- **Stone Warden:** an **Earth Elemental**, with HP raised to 180 and a **Shell**: while its shell stands, it has **Resistance to all damage**. Legendary Action (1/round): **Shatter Ground**, collapsing one rock island it's touching (Dex save DC 15, or fall into the lava stream: 6d10 fire, then climb out).

**Core mechanic** *(as designed)*
- **Scorching Charge** drops wherever the Flame Warden's attacks land. Load it into a **ballista** (an action), then fire at the Stone Warden: **+8 to hit, 4d10 fire**, and its **shell is stripped** until the end of its next turn.
- **Stone Hide** drops wherever the Stone Warden's attacks land. Either:
  - **Wear it:** fire resistance and safe lava walking for 1 minute, to melee the Flame Warden directly, or
  - **Feed a channeling rod** (an action): the Flame Warden's attacks deal half damage and it loses its Legendary Actions until the end of its next turn.
- Both are single-use. Players have to keep going back for drops.
- **Salamanders (if Vistro's labor boon was chosen):** two salamander warriors can crew the ballistas. They still need the charges brought to them.

**On defeat**
> [!readaloud]
> The Flame Warden gutters out into a pool of light. The Stone Warden splits along a seam and falls in two. Where each one stood, something small and bright is left behind: a crystal of Soulvite, one burning, one dull as granite.
>
> Then, from under your feet, comes a sound none of you have heard down here before: a deep mechanical *thrum*, building. The heating cores roar. Lines of blue light race along the silver channels and up the walls, through the shafts, toward the city. The lights are coming on.

- **The fire and earth Soulvite shards.** With the air and water shards from the upper floors, the party now holds all four for the **Soulvite Confluence**. *(The Colossus vault is open to them whenever they're ready.)*
- **Power restored:** the elevator, the dock crane, the Forge's heat, and the lights on every floor they've visited.

### The Soulvite Forge

> [!readaloud]
> The forge is a cathedral of anvils. A dozen of them stand in a ring around a central hearth the size of a house, each with its own hammer hanging on a chain above it, and over the hearth hangs a great bell-shaped hood of silver and iron. Around the walls, racks of half-finished work: blade blanks, breastplates, a dwarf-sized helm with no face. The hearth is dark. Everything in the room is waiting for it.

**Bringing it online** *(needs the Power Station first)*
1. **Heat.** With power restored, a lever brings the hearth roaring up. Without power, nothing. (A fire-giver, like a salamander or Thimble with enough fire, can light it for one hour, once.)
2. **Calibration.** Above the hearth are three great dials: **Silver**, **Ley**, and **Element**. They have to be set to a ratio before the forge will take metal.
   - The chart from the Foundry shows two: **Clausius's 3:1** and **Vashara's 1:1**.
   - Without the chart: **Arcana DC 16** or **Smith's Tools DC 15** to work out a safe ratio by trial (on a failure, a blast of ley energy: 3d6 force to everyone at the hearth).
   - The ratio they choose sets how the forge works, every time, until someone changes it.

**Clausius's ratio (3:1): stable**
- Items come out clean and reliable.

**Vashara's ratio (1:1): the metal remembers**
- Items come out **one step stronger** (see below). But each one is a little bit *aware*. It warms to its wielder, hums when they're in danger, and hates being put down. The DM may give it one mild quirk from the **Sentient Items** rules (it has opinions). It's powerful, unsettling, and it's exactly what Vashara wanted.
- **Insight DC 13 (anyone holding a 1:1 item for the first time):** it feels like being *watched kindly*. Like the Shell.

**Crafting** *(once online)*
- Takes **1 hour of work** at the forge and a **Smith's Tools DC 15** check (Aalyn crafted Stormsong; let the party's smith shine). On a failure, the material isn't lost; try again in an hour.
- **Materials:** Soulvite Ingots (from the Foundry and the Silver Vault) and **minor Soulvite shards** (Alaric has two, the Foundry has one). **The four boss shards are not crafting material.** They're needed at the Confluence.

| Materials | Clausius (3:1) | Vashara (1:1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Ingot | **+1 Weapon**, **+1 Armor**, or **+1 Shield** | **+2 Weapon** or **+2 Shield** (with a quirk) |
| 1 Ingot + 1 minor shard | **Armor of Resistance** (the shard's element) or a **+1 Weapon** that deals +1d4 of the shard's element | The same, plus the wielder can sense hostile creatures of that element within 60 ft (with a quirk) |
| 2 Ingots | **+2 Weapon** or **+2 Armor** | **+3 Weapon** (with a quirk); only once, ever, and the forge cracks after |

- All re-flavored official items. In D&D Beyond, build them from the official item and add the Soulvite flavor text.

**Discoveries**
- **The faceless helm** on the rack: dwarven work, unfinished, with a socket inside the brow exactly the shape of the Mind Medallion's stone. **Investigation DC 14:** a note in Vashara's hand tucked inside it. *"For A. If I can't wake him, I'll make him a better face."*
- **The order ledger:** the forge's last commission, signed by the Merchant Alliance and countersigned by the Crown: *"Forty blades, one to one, delivery by the Darkening."* It was never delivered. *(The Gold Vault's charter will make this land.)*
