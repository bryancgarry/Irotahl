## 1. The Procession of the Hollow

**Map**
- Circular room, about 60 ft across, ringed around the open central shaft
- A large pale sphere floats at the center, over the shaft
- Five pillars around the edge, each topped with a small pale sphere
- Murals on the walls

> [!readaloud] Entering
> The stair delivers you into a round chamber, and for a moment the dark is broken. At its center, floating above the open shaft with nothing holding it, hangs a great sphere of pale stone, smooth as a pearl and softly glowing, like moonlight trapped under ice. Around the edge of the room stand five black pillars, and on each one rests a smaller sphere of the same pale stone, glowing faintly in time with the large one. Like breathing.
>
> Five pillars. Five of you.
>
> On the walls, the mural begins again.

### The Mural of the Mind

> [!readaloud] The first panel
> The Mother stands before the stone giant. A crown of black needles rises from her fingertips like a fan of quills. His eyes are open. He does not flinch.
> *A mind is a house full of words, and every word is a door that does not lead to her.*

> [!readaloud] The second panel
> The needles pierce his brow one by one. From the wounds, words pour out of him: carved as small birds, hundreds of them, lifting away from his head into the corners of the panel. Some of the birds are carved with tiny letters on their wings, in Giant.
> *She closed every door.*

> [!readaloud] The third panel
> His head is carved hollow now, a dark cavity. With a single needle, she writes one bright mark into the emptiness: her split heart.
> *And in the silence, she wrote her will.*

> [!readaloud] The fourth panel
> Pilgrims kneel in pairs before a great pale sphere. In each pair, one pilgrim's eyes are covered with a band of black, and the other, already hollow-eyed, leans close to whisper in their ear. Beyond them, the stone giant strides into battle, perfectly silent, perfectly sure.
> *He needed no words. He had hers. So shall you.*

**Rolls (murals)**
- **History DC 15 (second panel):** The letters on the birds' wings are Giant. Read together, they're fragments of a climbing chant, the kind the Goliaths still sing on Tyrn'Haka. *(Wayne has advantage. Don't explain why it would be here.)*
- **Insight DC 13 (fourth panel):** The blindfolded pilgrims aren't afraid. They've been taught to trust the voice in their ear more than their own eyes. That's the lesson.
- **Religion DC 14 (fourth panel):** A hollowing rite. The pilgrim gives up their own sight and their own judgment, one step at a time, until only someone else's voice is left.
- **Arcana DC 13 (the spheres):** The small spheres are bound to the large one. Whatever is inside them is meant to end up inside it.

**The inscription around each pillar** *(Abyssal, translated)*
> *Pilgrim, do not trust your eyes. Do not trust your mind. Trust only the voice that leads you, until you need no voice at all.*

> [!info] DM Note
> **The story beat:** the mind is hollowed by surrendering it. Pilgrims came in pairs, one already hollow to guide, one trying to become hollow. The pilgrim walked a path they couldn't see, trusting only the voice in their ear. The faithful pilgrim ends up trusting only the Mother's voice. The party ends up trusting each other's. That's the whole trial.

### Entering the Mindscape

When a character touches a small sphere, their body goes still where they stand, eyes open and glowing faintly pale, and their mind is pulled inside. All five have to go in for the trial to begin. Myra, Rec, and anyone else not on a pillar stay in the room and watch five bodies standing frozen.

> [!readaloud] The mindscape
> The room is gone.
>
> You stand at the edge of a vast black void, on a single tile of pale stone. Far ahead, at the center of everything, hangs the great pale sphere, enormous now, glowing like a moon. Between you and it runs a path of pale stone tiles, leading straight and solid toward the center.
>
> You can see the others, each at the start of their own path. Five paths, all converging on the sphere like spokes on a wheel. Like needles.
>
> And walking the paths with you, faint as breath on glass, are pilgrims. Ghosts of the ones who came before, demon-shaped and hollow-eyed, shuffling toward the center with black bands over their eyes. Some of them are frozen mid-step on tiles that aren't there, mouths open in silent screams as they fall forever into the dark. Small pale birds drift off their shoulders and vanish.
>
> Then you notice something about the other paths. Theirs look different from yours. Broken. Winding. Full of gaps that aren't on your own.

### How It Works

- **Your own path is a lie.** Each walker sees their own path as a solid, straight line of tiles to the center. Most of those tiles are false.
- **You can see one other path truly.** Each player sees the real path of one other player. Send each guide a private image of that grid (VTT whisper or Discord DM).
- **The loop:**

| Player | Sees the true path of |
| --- | --- |
| Aalyn | Alaric |
| Alaric | Bogtrotter |
| Bogtrotter | Thimble |
| Thimble | Wayne |
| Wayne | Aalyn |

*If a player is absent, remove their pillar. Whoever would have guided them guides the next player in the loop instead.*

**Each round**
1. Go around the table. Each guide gives their walker one instruction of **up to three steps**.
2. The walker moves their token step by step. Steps are orthogonal only (forward, back, left, right).
3. Everyone can speak. Guides can say anything they want, but they can't hand over the image.

**A false step**
> [!readaloud]
> The tile under your foot is not there. It never was. You drop into the black, and the needles find you on the way down.

- **2d8 psychic damage** (DC 14 Wisdom saving throw for half).
- The walker reappears at the start of their path. Tiles they've already stood on stay faintly lit for them.
- **The needle takes a word.** You pick one word that player can't say out loud until the trial ends: "left," "right," "forward," "back," "step," "two," a character's name. If they say it anyway, they take 1d4 psychic damage. This is the hollowing happening to them at the table, and it makes them a worse guide with every fall.

**The false voice**
Once per walker during the trial, you can privately send them a fake instruction in their guide's voice. Agreeing on a code word beats it. Reward that.

**Alaric, the illusionist**
- Once per round, Alaric can cast an illusion onto any tile on any path. If the tile is false, the two illusions clash and it flickers, revealing it. *(Minor Illusion works. Count it as his action for the round, after he gives his own instruction.)*
- When a false voice is sent to anyone, Alaric can make an **Intelligence (Arcana) check DC 14**. On a success, he hears the seam in it and knows which instruction was false.

### The Obstacles

Each path has one obstacle: an illusion standing on a **true** tile. It's drawn from the walker's own mind. It blocks the way forward and points them somewhere else.

**Running an obstacle**
- When a walker's step would enter the obstacle's tile, the illusion takes hold and speaks to them.
- The illusion always beckons toward a **false** tile beside it. If the walker follows it, they fall.
- To step through it, the walker makes a **DC 14 Wisdom saving throw**. On a failure, they hesitate: the rest of their instruction is lost, and they take 1d8 psychic damage. They can try again next round.
- **Ways past without the save:** an **Intelligence (Investigation) DC 14** check to see through it, Alaric's illusion clashing with it (which dissolves it), or a guide giving the walker a reason to keep walking that you judge good enough (advantage, or an automatic success).
- Once the walker steps through it, it's gone.

- Same circular footprint
- The shaft is filled to the brim with the Sea of Void
- A huge black stone greatsword stands point-first in the void's surface, its hilt within reach of the edge
- Set in the center of the hilt: a large refined pale sphere (the soul)
- Murals on the walls

> [!readaloud] Entering
> The shaft here isn't empty. It's full to the brim with black liquid, glassy and still, a well with no bottom. Standing in it, driven point-first into its surface as if it were stone, is a greatsword of black stone taller than a giant. Its hilt is wrapped in grey leather gone hard as rock, and set into the center of the crossguard is a great sphere of pale stone, polished smooth, glowing softly from within. Words are carved down the length of the blade.
>
> As you come near, each of you notices a faint pale light glowing in your own chest, white as bone and warm as a hearth. You see it in each other's, too. And the sphere in the hilt glows in time with all of them.

> [!info] DM Note: what the sphere is
> The sphere is **a soul**: the pale, whole soul every pilgrim brings into this room, the thing the mural shows being drawn out and made dark. It is **not** an Ember and plays no Ember role in the trial. The only Ember material on this floor is the first two mural panels, which are a lore reference: the pale seed is the **Shard of Evil**, the original Ember of Sacrifice, and the one who catches it and "becomes the Deep" is Tharizdun. The pale stones the Deep weeps up are the Embers, fragments of that first one. Keep Tharizdun unnamed.

**Carved down the blade** *(translated)*
> *A blade does not hate. A blade does not grieve. A blade does not hesitate. And so a blade may do what no living soul could ever bear.*

### The Mural of the Soul

> [!readaloud] The first panel
> A black sky beyond the stars. From it falls a single pale seed, and the one who catches it becomes the Deep.
> *The first offering was made before any altar.*

> [!readaloud] The second panel
> The Deep weeps small pale stones up into the world. A figure holds one aloft, and around them kneel those they offered, each bearing a mark on their skin.
> *Those offered are marked, and the mark is a promise: what is pale shall be made dark.*

> [!readaloud] The third panel
> A pale hall of hollowers, each with a triangle and a pupil-less eye stitched on their robes. The stone giant is led in among them, quiet and willing.
> *In the Sanctum, the Mother's hollowers made ready her beloved.*

> [!readaloud] The fourth panel
> The hollowers draw a pale, curled soul out of his open chest on a thread of light and hold it up in a cage of glass.
> *What is pale must be emptied.*

> [!readaloud] The fifth panel
> The pale soul lowered into a black sea, raised again black as the sea itself, and set back into the hollow of his chest.
> *So was his soul made dark, and made hers.*

**Rolls (murals)**
- **No roll (second panel):** The marks are carved exactly like the party's brands. Everyone's brand itches while they look at it.
- **Religion DC 14 (second panel):** The pale stones and the marks are bound together. Whoever holds a stone and offers someone, the offered are marked.
- **Arcana DC 15 (first and second panels):** The pale stones sound like the Embers of Sacrifice. Like the pearl Atul described Vashara holding to her ear.
- **Insight DC 13:** "What is pale shall be made dark." It reads like a promise about *your* souls.
- **Wayne (third panel, no roll):** The sigil on the hollowers' robes. Again.

### The Ritual

**How it starts.** The trial begins when the pilgrims lay their hands on the pale sphere in the hilt together. Anyone who doesn't touch it stays at the edge and watches the others stand frozen with their eyes gone white.

> [!readaloud] When they touch the sphere
> The moment your hands rest on the sphere together, the light in your chests flares, and the sphere drinks it in. The blade sinks. It slides into the black as smoothly as into a sheath, and it takes you with it.
>
> There is no drowning. There is no cold. There is only dark, and then the dark has a shape, and the shape is *you*, enormous and made of stone, standing on a mountain that floats over a river of fire.
>
> You are him. And you remember.

**The three depths** *(each a memory of the Shield-Halls; each player makes a secret choice at each depth)*

**Secret choices online.** At each depth, after you read the scene, each player privately sends you **Blade** or **Self** (VTT whisper or Discord DM). Wait until all five are in, resolve them, and narrate vaguely. Don't reveal who chose what until the end.

> [!readaloud] The first depth: Hate
> A great hall burns around you, its roof beams falling in sheets of flame. Before you stands a giant chieftain, grey-bearded, his shield split, his own blood in his beard. He knows your face. He spits at your feet.
>
> "Traitor to your blood. Thief wearing a prince's skin. Your father will curse your name until the mountains fall."
>
> Your hand is already rising.
> *A blade does not hate.*

> [!readaloud] The second depth: Grief
> The field after. The dead lie in their thousands, and out of every one of them, a pale light rises and pours into your chest, filling you, and filling you. Around you, the living kneel among their dead: a mother with her son's head in her lap, an old giant rocking a body twice his size. They wail. You walk through them.
> *A blade does not grieve.*

> [!readaloud] The third depth: Hesitation
> The last of them. A young shield-maiden stands alone in a narrow doorway, shield up, spear shaking. Behind her, in the dark of the hall, you hear children trying very hard to be quiet. She doesn't run. She looks up at you, and for one moment, she looks *hopeful*, as if she thinks you might still be someone she knew.
>
> Raise your hand.
> *A blade does not hesitate.*

**The choice at each depth**
- **Become the blade.** Let it go. Your light dims one step, and the void lets you pass untouched.
- **Remain yourself.** Hold on. Your light stays, but the void bears down on you: a **Wisdom saving throw** (DC 13, then 15, then 17), taking **2d8, then 3d8, then 4d8 psychic damage** on a failure.

> [!info] DM Note
> Reveal choices only at the end. Narrate each depth's result vaguely ("the memory moves on without you") so no one knows who chose what until the lights are shown.

**What remains**
> [!readaloud]
> At the bottom, there is nothing. No hall, no field, no doorway. Only dark, and the faint light in your chest, however much of it is left.
>
> The void asks each of you one question, and it asks in your own voice.
>
> *What is left of you?*

- Each player answers in one sentence, in character.
- Then everyone sees everyone's light for the first time: bright, dim, or gone.

**Aalyn:** before the question, the void asks him first, in his patron's voice for just one word: *"You are already promised. To whom?"*

**Wayne:** his light gutters at every depth, no matter what he chooses, but it never goes out. The Reliquary doesn't explain why. *(At the bottom, for him alone, the dark around him is not quite empty. Somewhere very far off, he hears many voices at once, deep and slow, too far to make out. Then they're gone. Do not explain this.)*

**The Tome:** if Thimble carries the Tome of Malcanthet into the descent, it drinks the light he gives up. **Note it.** Malcanthet arrives at the Seat knowing who has her Tome.

**The outcome**
> [!readaloud]
> The dark lets go of you. You're standing at the edge of the well again, dry, breathing hard.
>
> No. The blade is still there. It never moved. But the sphere in its hilt has changed. It was pale when you touched it. Now it is black, black as the well, and as you watch, a hairline crack opens around its middle. From the crack, something small and dark slides free and drops into your hands: the third sigil piece.
>
> *What is pale shall be made dark.*

- **Characters whose light went out** (chose Blade at all three depths): for the rest of the Reliquary, advantage on saving throws against being charmed or frightened, and weapon attacks deal an extra 1d6 necrotic damage. *Cost:* their brand darkens permanently, and Malcanthet knows their name. **These characters also pass through the Soul stage of the Collapse untouched.**
- **Characters who held on** keep themselves, wounds and all.

## 7. The Shell of the Void Heart

**The choice.** No one makes Wayne take it. Let the table talk. If Wayne doesn't take it, anyone else can, but the curse only bites a would-be vessel. For anyone else, it's an inert, faintly warm stone.

**The item** *(D&D Beyond-ready)*
```

Wondrous item, very rare (requires attunement). Cursed.

The broken shell of a giant's pendant, grey stone worn smooth by a climber's hand, its setting cracked open. Inside, a bead of black void moves on its own, rising into tiny spikes whenever someone nearby thinks too hard.

You can use Shade Step a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest. Each use beyond that gives you 1 Hollowing.

Kin of the Void. The Sea of Void doesn't bristle at you. Hollowed creatures hesitate before attacking you: the first attack each one makes against you in a combat has disadvantage.

- At 3 Hollowing, Malcanthet can whisper to you through the Shell at will. Her words are suggestions only. You can always refuse.
- At 5 Hollowing, your mind is hollowed. Once per long rest, Malcanthet can cast command on you through the Shell, with no saving throw.

Homebrew note: Shade Step and Kin of the Void are homebrew, inspired by Hollow Knight's Shade Cloak, Sharp Shadow, and Void Heart. The curse follows the DMG's cursed item conventions.
```

# MOVEMENT FOUR: THE COLLAPSE

## 8. The House Goes Home

> [!readaloud] The moment the Shell leaves the Seat
> Nothing happens. For one long breath, nothing at all.
>
> Then every frozen figure on the field turns its head.
>
> Not toward you. *Down.* Toward the red-black sky beneath your feet, where the clouds have begun to turn, slowly, like water circling a drain. The wind rises. A floating slab of mountain at the field's edge tips, groans, and falls away into the red, and the hundreds of stone giants on it go with it, without a sound.
>
> The whole house shudders like something waking. And deep in the stone, the heartbeat you heard in the nave starts again, faster, faster, until it isn't a heartbeat anymore. It's a drum.
>
> The house is going home. And you are in it.

### How the Escape Works

The party must climb back up through every floor: the Field, the Sealed Point, Soul, Mind, Body and the Stair, the Turning, and the Nave, then out through the doors. Run it as **six stages**.

**The Collapse Clock: 8 boxes.**
- Each stage fills **1 box** no matter what.
- A failed stage fills **1 extra box**.
- The party can afford **two failures** and still walk out the doors on their own.

**Each stage:**
1. Read the stage description.
2. Each player describes how their character gets through, then makes the check you name, or any other check they can justify. **DC 14.**
3. **Group check:** if at least half the party succeeds, the stage succeeds.
4. **Each individual failure** takes the stage's cost, whether the group succeeded or not.

**Running notes**
- Keep it fast. One pass around the table per stage.
- Reward creative help. One character spending their action to help another grants advantage, and a good idea can be an automatic success.
- **Wayne's Shade Step** auto-succeeds a stage for himself and can carry one ally through with him (the ally passes inside his shadow, unharmed). Every use past his proficiency bonus gives him 1 Hollowing. Make the temptation clear without pushing it.
- Myra, if present, slows everyone down a little and has to be carried in at least one stage. She's worth it.

### Stage 1: The Falling Field
> [!readaloud]
> The Field is coming apart. Slabs of floating mountain tilt and slide away into the red beneath you, one after another, faster. The path back to the Sealed Point is a broken chain of falling rock, and the gaps between them are growing.

- **Check:** Athletics or Acrobatics.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage, landing hard on the next slab.
- **The Ashen Reach:** if the party never broke off the sage's pillar shard, one player can grab it on the way past with a failure's risk but no check penalty.

### Stage 2: The Overflowing Well
> [!readaloud]
> You haul yourselves up through the Sealed Point and into the Chamber of Soul, and the well is overflowing. The Sea of Void is pouring out of the shaft and across the floor, rising into spikes, needles, reaching hands. It reaches for every light in the room.

- **Check:** Dexterity saving throw, or Wisdom saving throw to hold yourself steady while it reaches.
- **Cost of failure:** 2d8 psychic damage as the void brushes your light.
- **Characters whose light went out** pass untouched. The void doesn't notice them anymore. Let that be unsettling.

### Stage 3: The Cracked Sphere
> [!readaloud]

## 9. Vashara

**The situation**
- The island is Reliquary stone. With the house gone, it's breaking apart and sinking into the lava lake, slowly, a few feet at a time.
- Atul's petrified army stands on it. They're going down with it.
- The fire sages are spent and collapsed on their pillars. The salamanders' rafts are on their way, but not fast enough to save an army of statues.
- **Vashara is already here.** She teleported in the moment she felt the stitch snap.

> [!readaloud] The arrival
> The island is breaking apart beneath your feet. Long cracks run through the black glass, and lava wells up through them, orange in the dark. At the edge of the Field of Stone, the first dwarven soldiers tip, slowly, and slide beneath the surface without a sound.
>
> At the center of the field, someone is kneeling beside the stone king.
>
> She is drow, tall and fine-boned, in a gown of deep violet that's scorched along the hem from crossing the fire. Her white hair is loose. Folded at her back, almost hidden, are a pair of thin leathery wings. She has both arms wrapped around Atul's stone waist, and she is trying to pull him. As if she could drag a statue across a sinking island by herself. As if she's forgotten she could do anything else.
>
> She hears you. She doesn't let go. She doesn't even turn around.
>
> "You took it out," she says. Her voice is low and smoky and very familiar. "Of course you did. She'd have wanted you to."

**Recognition**
- **No roll for anyone who's been to V's Lounge:** the voice. It's V.
- **Bogtrotter:** the perfume. Same as the lounge. Something with smoke and lilac in it.
- **Insight DC 12:** She isn't here for you. She hasn't looked at you once.
- **Perception DC 14:** There's a pale stone on a fine chain around her neck, tucked under the gown. Pearl-like. *(Her Ember.)*
- **Investigation DC 13 (if the party saw the alcove):** *Forgive me, Atul.* Same hand.

> [!info] DM Note: the shape of this scene
> **Vashara is not here to fight.** She's here to save Atul's body, and she's run out of time and ideas. What she wants from the party is the **Mind Medallion**: she wants to hear him speak to her one more time. She has bargaining chips, she has pride, and she has a very short fuse if anyone threatens the statue. When the scene ends, she teleports away. Whether she takes Atul with her is the party's call.

### Her Goals and Leverage

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **She wants** | One last conversation with Atul through the Mind Medallion. Then to take his body somewhere it won't sink. |
| **She won't do** | Fight the party (Malcanthet's favorite is standing right there), hand over her Ember, or apologize to anyone but Atul. |
| **She can offer** | **Bastian Dray** (alive, beneath her lounge, released unharmed) or **a warning about the Temple of 1000 Candles** (one of the ritual's anchor points, given freely). |
| **She fears** | That Atul will curse her. That he won't. |
| **Clock** | The island sinks. Give the scene about **5 to 7 exchanges** before the statue's feet are in the lava. |

### Dialog

**Opening**
- **(Still holding him):** "He weighs as much as a mountain. Did you know that? I used to tease him that he was the only thing in Durnhollow I couldn't lift."
- **(Turning at last, eyes on the medallion):** "You have his voice. In your pocket. You woke him once, I felt it. You gave him back his words and you let him talk to *salamanders.*"
- **(The ask):** "Give him to me. A minute. That's all that medallion has left in it, I know exactly what it is. I made it."

**If the party asks who she is**
- "You know who I am. You've been drinking my wine for weeks." *(A tired almost-smile.)* "Queen Vashara of Durnhollow. The cleverest mind in the city. Ask anyone. They're all dead, but ask them."

**If asked about Malcanthet or the Ember**
- "She found me in that house the way she found your friend in his ruins. The difference is, I said yes." *(A beat.)* "I said yes and I would say it again, and I am still on my knees on this rock. Make of that what you like."

**If asked why she petrified Atul**
- "Because he was going to stop me. And because if I'd let him into that house, it would have kept him." *(Quiet.)* "Stone was the kindest thing I had."

**To Wayne (she looks at the Shell, if he carries it)**
- "She gave you a piece of *him.*" *(Something bitter crosses her face.)* "I gave her a kingdom. She gave me a stone that whispers." *(Softer, almost pitying.)* "Be careful, Goliath. She's very generous at the start."

**To Thimble (the medallion's holder, likely)**
- "He said you were kind to him. Little one. He called you little one, didn't he? He used to call me that, and I'm taller than he is."

**If threatened, or if anyone moves to stop her from touching the statue**
- *(Everything goes still around her. Her eyes go white.)* "Don't. I am asking you very nicely, and I don't ask anyone nicely anymore."
- If someone actually attacks: she petrifies the attacker's weapon arm in an instant (no roll; it reverts at the end of the scene) and says, "That was a warning. You've seen what I do when I stop warning." She does not escalate further. If attacked again, she teleports away immediately, alone, and the statue sinks.

### Rolls

- **Persuasion DC 13:** She'll bargain. She offers one of her chips (the party's choice) in exchange for the medallion.
- **Persuasion DC 17, or a truly good argument:** She offers both.
- **Insight DC 14:** She's terrified. Not of you. Of what he'll say.
- **Intimidation:** doesn't work. She's been afraid of worse things than this party for a thousand years. On a 20 or higher, she lets out a real laugh: "Oh, I *like* you. Fine. Ask for something."
- **Deception:** she spent centuries in Malcanthet's service and runs a lounge full of liars. Disadvantage.

### If the Medallion Is Used

> [!readaloud]
> Light cracks through the stone, thin as a hair. It runs across his face, down his throat, along the outstretched arm. His eyes move first, grinding. His jaw. Dust falls from his beard.
>
> And this time, the first thing he sees is her.

**Atul and Vashara** *(play both; the party watches; keep it short, it's a minute)*
- **Atul:** "...Vash."
- **Vashara:** "I'm here. I'm here, love."
- **Atul:** "The doors. Did you go in?"
- **Vashara:** *(A long breath.)* "Yes."
- **Atul:** *(He closes his eyes.)* "How many, Vash? Since. How many people?"
- **Vashara:** *(She doesn't answer.)*
- **Atul:** "The foundries. The ones you take. Let them go. Whatever she gave you, whatever you're making. Let them go." *(He reaches for her face with a cracked stone hand.)* "You were the cleverest mind in Durnhollow. You don't need her for anything."
- **Vashara:** *(Barely.)* "I can't."
- **Atul:** *(Gently, without anger.)* "Then I can't forgive you. Not yet." *(Pause.)* "But I'll wait."
- **The light dims. His hand stays on her cheek, stone again.**

> [!info] DM Note
> Atul never curses her and never absolves her. "I'll wait" is the hook. If the party later destroys her work, frees the foundry captives, or pulls her away from Malcanthet, that's the forgiveness she's waiting on. It makes her redeemable without making her safe.

**Vashara, after**
- *(She stays there, cheek against his hand, for a long moment. Then, without looking at the party, steady again.)* "Your price."
- She pays what was agreed. **Bastian Dray:** "Bastian is alive. He's under my lounge, in the cold room behind the wine. He'll walk out tomorrow night with all his fingers. You have my word, which is worth more than you'd think." **The Temple:** she names one anchor point of the lycanthropy ritual. *(Pick one of the five that suits your next Hollowmere session.)*

### If the Party Refuses the Medallion

- She doesn't beg twice. *(Very quietly.)* "Then I'll remember his face the way it was when I stilled it. That's fair. That's more than fair."
- She still tries to take the statue (see below), and she leaves no gift. She remembers who said no.

### Her Exit

**The party's choice, whether they realize it or not: does she take Atul?**
- **If they let her:** she lays her hand on his chest, and they vanish together in a fold of violet light, with a smell of smoke and lilac. *(He ends up beneath V's Lounge. The party can find him there later, and so can his army's story.)*
- **If they stop her** (stand between her and the statue, or get the salamanders to haul him onto a raft): she lets them, after a long look. "Then you keep him. Keep him well. If I find him at the bottom of this lake, I'll know whose fault it was." She vanishes alone.
- **If they do nothing either way:** she takes him.

> [!readaloud] Last words, to the whole party
> "You know where to find me. You always have."
>
> *(To Wayne, if he has the Shell, just before the light takes her.)* "Sweet thing. You'll come back to her. Everyone does."
